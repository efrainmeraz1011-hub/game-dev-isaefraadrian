# Marine nationale battleships and battlecruisers, 1939 to 1945

In September 1939 the Marine nationale had five capital ships in first-line service: the three Bretagne class battleships completed between 1915 and 1916 [R3] and the two Dunkerque class battlecruisers completed 1 May 1937 and 6 April 1939 [R2]. Three Courbet class ships of 1913 to 1914 were still on the list in training roles, Courbet as a gunnery training ship and Paris as a signal training ship since 1931, and Océan, the former Jean Bart, as a disarmed training hulk from late 1937 [R4]. One ship was completed during the war, Richelieu on 15 July 1940 [R1]; her sister Jean Bart left Saint-Nazaire incomplete for Casablanca on 19 to 22 June 1940 and was not finished until after the war [R1]. The fleet was then destroyed in two separate events, and each ship's fate is stated below from a source rather than from the event. At Mers-el-Kébir on 3 July 1940 Operation Catapult put three battleships out of action and heavily damaged two more, and cost about 1,300 French lives [R8]; Bretagne alone lost 944 men after four shell hits from Hood, Resolution and Valiant [R6]. At Toulon on 27 November 1942 close to ninety ships totalling 235,000 tons were scuttled, among them the battlecruisers Strasbourg and Dunkerque and the older battleship Provence [R7]. Courbet was scuttled as a breakwater off Arromanches on 9 June 1944 [R4], and the incomplete Clemenceau was sunk by American aircraft at Brest on 27 August 1944 [R1].

## Conflicts between sources

- Which British ships sank Bretagne. navypedia names the battleship Resolution [R3]; the Service historique de la Défense names Hood, Resolution and Valiant and gives four shell hits [R6].
- Casualties at Mers-el-Kébir. The Service historique de la Défense event page gives about 1,300 French dead and three battleships out of action with two more heavily damaged [R8]; the same service's record on Bretagne gives nearly 1,300 dead of which 944 were from Bretagne [R6].
- Which capital ships were scuttled at Toulon. The Ministère des Armées page names Strasbourg, Dunkerque and Provence [R7]; navypedia adds the disarmed hulk Océan, scuttled the same day [R4].
- Océan's renaming. The navypedia ships table gives January 1937 [R4]; the naval service text on the same page gives 1 January 1937 and dates the conversion to a training hulk to late 1937 [R4].
