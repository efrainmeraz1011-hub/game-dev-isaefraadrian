# HMS Howe (pennant 32)

Ordered as Beatty and renamed on 21 February 1940 [S5], [S23]. Last ship of the class to complete, first to receive Bofors, and the ship whose machinery spaces and gun positions were photographed in the most detail on completion. The timeline is in facet 08.

## Completion

Built by Fairfield at Govan, laid down 1 June 1937, launched 9 April 1940, completed 29 August 1942 [S23]. No work was done on her for six months after May 1940 because higher-priority repair work took the yard [S5]. She commissioned for trials on 17 June 1942, completed a radar fit of 14 sets at Rosyth by August 1942, and made 27.5 knots on her full-power trial of 2 September 1942 [S5].

As the last ship completed she carried the heaviest as-built close-range battery in the class. The tier 3 monograph gives her as built with 48 pom-pom barrels in six octuple mountings and 18 single 20 mm guns, against King George V's 32 barrels in four octuple mountings and four UP launchers, with a standard displacement of 39,450 tons, deep load of 42,530 tons, 112,930 shp (84.2 MW) for 27.5 knots, and a complement of 1,556 [S69].

## Service

Home Fleet and Arctic cover through 1942 and into 1943 [S5]. In July 1943 she joined Operation Husky, bombarding Trapani on 12 July with King George V, and in September 1943 took part in the Taranto operation and escorted the surrendered Italian fleet [S5], [S1]. She went to the Eastern Fleet in August 1944, to the British Pacific Fleet in November 1944, and to Sydney in December 1944, and holds Okinawa as a battle honour [S5].

## Devonport refit, December 1943 to May 1944

Type 274 replaced Type 284, and her aircraft facilities were removed [S5]. Navypedia dates the refit to May 1944 and gives the fuller result: six single Oerlikons, the catapult with its seaplanes, and Type 273, Type 281 and Type 284 removed; two quadruple 40 mm Bofors, two octuple 2-pdr mountings, four twin Oerlikons, and Type 274, Type 277, Type 281B, two Type 282, Type 285 and Type 293 added, with full displacement rising to 45,226 t [S23]. That refit made her the first of the class to receive Bofors [S23].

By September 1945 navypedia records a further change of 34 single Oerlikons removed in exchange for 18 single 40 mm Bofors and six quadruple 2-pdr mountings [S23]; the Mark VII*P quadruple pom-pom mountings she then carried, six of them, were the late-war type with self-contained power and dual joystick control [S9].

## Conflicts between sources

- The date her aircraft facilities and Type 284 came out. Her chronology gives the refit as December 1943 to May 1944 [S5]; navypedia dates the change to May 1944 [S23].
- Her as-built standard displacement. 39,450 tons [S69], against 38,031 tons for King George V in 1940 [S18]; no source found states the cause of the difference.
