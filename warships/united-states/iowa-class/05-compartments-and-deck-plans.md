# Iowa class: compartments and deck plans

Status: partial (see gaps-05.md)

Source numbers refer to `sources-05.md`. Ships covered: Iowa (BB-61) and her three completed sisters; the deck plans read here are the Iowa booklet, whose title block gives the class hull numbers only on the 1954 damage control sheet, which is headed BB 61-64 [S193].

## Documents behind this facet

Three document sets carry almost everything below.

The **1984 Booklet of General Plans for BB-61** is the only complete set of deck plans reached in this session. The Internet Archive item lists it as created by Navy Yard, Brooklyn, New York, dated 1984, and its description marks plates 01 to 14 as 1984 work and the cover photograph as 17 October 1987 [S182]. Each plate carries Bu. No. 578106 and the scale 1/16" = 1'-0" [S186] [S190] [S184]. **Every space taken from these plates is marked as 1984 work.** The deck headings in section 4 carry "(1984 booklet)", and every citation to S183 through S192 anywhere in this file is a citation to a 1984 plate. The booklet shows the ship after the 1980s reactivation, so it is evidence for the 1984 arrangement and only indirect evidence for 1943 to 1945. Section 7 lists what is known to differ.

The **1954 damage control diagram 1, liquid loading** (BB 61-64), prepared by New York Naval Shipyard for the Bureau of Ships in December 1954 and declassified under EO 13526 section 3.3, is the intermediate document. It carries an inboard profile lettered with compartment numbers, twenty four transverse sections, a first platform plan and an inner bottom plan [S193].

**Nomenclature of Naval Vessels**, revised February 1942 and first printed June 1942 by the Navy Department's Division of Personnel Supervision and Management with the Bureau of Ships, is the rule book behind the deck names, the compartment numbers and the door and hatch numbers used on both drawings [S181]. HyperWar's transcription of the Bluejackets' Manual is the 1940 tenth edition, not the 1944 edition, and the chapter files under that directory returned 404 on the date of access, so nothing from the Bluejackets' Manual is cited here [S195].

The **docking plan**, drawing BB616-0700AA sheet 1 of the Planning Division, Navy Yard New York, marked Preliminary and covering BB-61 to BB-64, gives the frame spacing and the transverse section at frame 121 [S194].

## 1. Deck naming and numbering

Nomenclature of Naval Vessels sets the convention for every US Navy ship of the period. A complete deck runs the full length of the ship; a partial deck runs only part of it. Decks are named by their position above or below the main deck, which is the highest deck running from stem to stern [S181]. A complete deck below the main deck is the second deck; where there are two or more, they are the second deck, third deck, fourth deck and so on. A partial deck below the lowest complete deck is a platform, and where there are several the one immediately below the lowest complete deck is the first platform, the next the second platform, and so on [S181]. A partial deck above the main deck that does not reach the ship's side is a superstructure deck [S181]. Decks given plating of extra strength and thickness for protection are further defined as "protective" and "splinter" in addition to their regular names; where there is one such deck it is the protective deck, and where there are two the lower one is the splinter deck [S181].

The class follows that scheme exactly. The 1954 inboard profile labels the levels, downward from the main deck: main deck, second deck, third deck, first platform, second platform, third platform, hold, third bottom, inner bottom, base line [S193]. Upward from the main deck the same profile labels: first superstructure deck, second superstructure deck, flag and signal bridge, navigating bridge, pilot house top level, then first, second, third, fourth, fifth and sixth level above pilot house top [S193].

The 1984 booklet issues one plate per level and uses the numeric level names for the superstructure. Its plate sequence is: general dimensions and data, outboard profile, inboard profile, bridges, first and superstructure deck, main deck, second deck, splinter deck, third deck, first platform, second and third platform, hold, third bottom [S182]. Plate 6 carries two plans, one titled "01 LEVEL" and one lettered with 0200-series compartments [S185], and plate 5 carries nine separate plans titled 03 LEVEL through 011 LEVEL [S184]. The 1984 inboard profile still prints the older words on the profile itself: "FLAG & SIGNAL BRIDGE", "NAV BRIDGE", "PILOT HOUSE", "1ST SUPER DK", "2ND SUPER DK", and "1ST LVL ABV P.H. TOP" [S183].

## 2. Frame numbering and compartment designation

### Frames

Nomenclature of Naval Vessels states that frames are numbered from forward aft, that the zero point is the forward perpendicular, a design point on the stem, that the first frame aft of it is frame 1, and that numbering runs consecutively to the after perpendicular [S181]. Frames are usually evenly spaced, but are set closer together in some sections, for instance at the very bow, for strength [S181]. Units aboard are located fore and aft by distance from the nearest frame, up or down by distance above or below a named deck or the base line, and athwartships by distance from the centreline [S181].

The docking plan's principal dimensions block gives frame spacing as 4'-0" and length between perpendiculars as 860'-0", and adds that the midship section is 24" aft of frame 107 [S194]. The 1984 deck plates carry a centreline frame scale ticked at every frame and numbered every five frames; on the main deck plate that scale runs from the stem to frame 215 and beyond, with the after perpendicular at the stern [S186]. The 1954 profile's scale runs from the forward perpendicular to the after perpendicular, printing AP at the aft end and numbering back through frames 210, 205, 200 and so on [S193].

In the principal living compartments and elsewhere along the ship's length, every fifth frame is numbered on the beams in a conspicuous place along passageways, as near the bottom edge of a beam as practicable, in accordance with General Specifications Appendix 10 [S181].

### Compartment designation

A watertight compartment's label has three parts: a division letter, a number, and one or more usage letters, written for example A-305-L. Nomenclature of Naval Vessels gives each part [S181].

**Division letter.** The ship is divided into three principal divisions, lettered A, B and C from forward aft [S181]. Division A runs from the stem to the forward transverse bulkhead of the forward machinery compartment; division B from that bulkhead to the after transverse bulkhead of the after machinery compartment; division C is everything aft of that [S181]. "Machinery compartment" means firerooms, boiler rooms, engine rooms, main motor rooms, main machinery spaces, and compartments holding auxiliaries of the main propelling machinery [S181]. The divisions run from the keel to the highest deck in line with the bulkheads or the bulkheads prolonged [S181].

On this class the boundaries are drawn and labelled on the plans. The main deck, second deck, third deck, first platform, second platform and hold plates all print a small mark on the frame scale reading `"B" DIVISION | "A" DIVISION` and `"C" DIVISION | "B" DIVISION` [S186] [S187] [S189] [S190] [S191] [S192]. The second deck plate puts the first at OT WT BHD FR 87 and the second at OT WT BHD FR 151 [S187]; the third deck and first platform plates repeat both frames [S189] [S190].

Frame 87 is the forward bulkhead of fire room no. 1 and frame 151 the after bulkhead of engine room no. 4, which is exactly the rule the manual states [S181] [S190].

**Number.** Compartments in each division are numbered from the forward end of that division; starboard compartments take odd numbers and port compartments even numbers [S181]. Every space completely bounded by watertight, oiltight, airtight or fumetight structure is numbered [S181]. The hundreds digit gives the deck: main deck 101 to 199, second deck 201 to 299, third deck 301 to 399, first platform 401 to 499, second platform 501 to 599, and, if there are no more platforms, the hold 601 to 699 [S181]. Decks above the main deck take a leading zero, 0101 to 0199 for the next deck up, 0201 to 0299 for the one above that, and so on [S181]. Upper inner bottom compartments are numbered 801 to 899 and lower inner bottom compartments 901 to 999 [S181]. Spaces with no decks that run from the inner bottom or the shell through two or more deck spaces, such as protective layers, engine rooms, boiler rooms, deep peak tanks and fuel oil tanks on certain battleships, are numbered from 1 up to 100, with boiler and engine rooms given the lowest numbers: B-1, B-2, B-3 and so on [S181].

The class has one more platform than the manual's worked example, so the series run one higher at the bottom. The 1954 profile shows the sequence in the bow, reading down: A-201A on the second deck, A-301V on the third deck, A-401A on the first platform, A-501A on the second platform, A-601A on the third platform, A-701A in the hold, and A-901V in the inner bottom, with the peak tank A-1W running through all of them as a 1-to-100 series space [S193]. The 1984 plates use the same series: hold spaces are 700-series (A-708A, A-718E, C-701E) [S192], third platform spaces 600-series (A-608T, A-609E) [S190] [S191], and the machinery spaces are B-1-1 through B-8-1 in the 1-to-100 series [S190].

Where a numbered watertight compartment is divided again by airtight or fumetight bulkheads, a suffix is added: the manual's example is watertight compartment A-312L becoming A-312-1L to starboard and A-312-2L to port [S181]. The 1984 plates use suffixes heavily, for instance crew's quarters A-209-1L, A-210-1L and A-217-1L on the second deck [S187] and the sick bay group A-317-1L, A-317-2L, A-317-3L, A-317-4L and A-317-5L on the third deck [S189].

### Door, hatch and manhole numbers

Doors, hatches and manholes carry their own numbers on label plates. The number has two or three parts separated by hyphens: the first is the deck number, the next is the frame number just forward of the hinge of the door, and the last is the number of the door where there is more than one on the same frame on that deck [S181]. Hatches and manholes take, for the first part, the number of the deck they are cut through [S181]. Odd numbers in the third part mean starboard, even mean port [S181]. The manual's examples: door 3-24-1 is on the third deck at or between frames 24 and 25 on the starboard side; door 3-24-2 is the port one; hatch 3-24-2 is on the third deck at or immediately aft of frame 24 on the port side [S181]. The label plate on a door carries three lines: the door number, the compartment name, and the compartment number, the manual's example being `4-16-2 / C.P.O. Stores / A-412` [S181].

The 1984 plates print such numbers. The second platform plate labels a forward watertight hatch H-5-157 and a hatch H-5-170 with gas over, both on that platform, the leading 5 being the deck count that reaches the second platform [S191]. The main deck plate labels a round watertight hatch 1-207 aft [S186].

### Longitudinal subdivision

The docking plan's section at frame 121, drawn at 1/8" = 1'-0" and labelled "showing extreme beam", names the longitudinal structure outboard from the centreline in order: centreline, longitudinal no. 1, longitudinal no. 2, docking longitudinal no. 3, longitudinal no. 4, longitudinal no. 5, holding bulkhead no. 4, armor bulkhead no. 3, torpedo bulkhead no. 2, torpedo bulkhead no. 1, then the shell [S194]. The same section letters the shell strakes with their plate weights, from the flat keel outward and upward: flat keel 80 pounds amidships, A 35, B 35, C 45, D 35, E 45, F 30, G 25, H 25, J 25, K 25, L 25, M 60, N 60, O 25, P 25, with the bilge keel between G and F and the normal waterline marked between M and N [S194]. Plate weight is weight per square foot of a given thickness; the manual's key is that a 5-pound plate is 1/8 inch thick, a 7 1/2-pound plate 3/16 inch, a 10-pound plate 1/4 inch and a 20-pound plate 1/2 inch [S181].

The docking plan also marks inner pier and outer pier blocking positions under the section, and note 9 of the general notes says that outboard piers under the E strake port and starboard are to be rearranged for the second and third docking positions to clear wing tank openings [S194]. Note 5 says frame 100 is the datum frame, placed over the centre of a three-block pier for the first docking, 24" forward of the pier centre for the second and 24" aft for the third, so that three successive dockings expose the entire shell [S194].

### Double and triple bottom

The manual defines a double bottom as construction with both an inner and an outer bottom, the spaces between being double bottom tanks, and says the arrangement protects against flooding if the outer bottom is holed [S181]. On this class there are two skins below the hold over part of the length. The 1954 sheet's deck list names, below the hold, a third bottom and then an inner bottom above the base line, and it carries a separate inner bottom plan [S193]. The 1984 booklet issues plate 14 as a separate "third bottom" plate [S182]. The 1984 hold plate carries the note "line of shell at level of 3rd skin FRS 140-171 P/S", which is where the third skin is drawn [S192], and a second note "line of shell at 2nd platf lvl FR 151-171" [S192]. The hold plate also prints "line of shell at 6'-0" W.L. P/S" over the machinery length [S192]. The inner bottom compartments carry 900-series numbers as the manual requires, for instance void C-935V, C-937V and C-938V and the rudder support assembly C-939V and cofferdam C-941V under the stern [S191] [S192] [S193].

## 6. Circulation

### Main passageways

Each deck is worked fore and aft by a numbered passage that carries the deck's compartment letter. On the main deck the through route is passage A-103L, which is labelled repeatedly from the officers' country forward of frame 88 to the after deck office near frame 148 [S186]. On the second deck the passages change letter with the division: passage A-210-1L and A-217-1LA forward, B-201L, B-202-2EL, B-208L, B-215A, B-218L, B-222-2L, B-225EL, B-226EL, B-227LA, B-231LA, B-234-2E, B-235LA and B-236LA through the shops, then C-207-LA, C-208-1L and C-210-2L aft [S187]. On the third deck the route is a chain of trunk-lettered passages: A-317-1L, A-330-1L, A-331L, A-338T, A-341T, A-351E and A-361E forward, B-301T, B-308T, B-308 1/2 T, B-324T, B-331A, B-340-2L, B-345T, B-354T, B-366T, B-367T, B-368T, B-375EA and B-376T through the machinery length, and C-301T, C-336L and C-337AL aft [S189]. The 1984 inboard profile draws these third-deck passages as a continuous run outboard of the fire rooms and engine rooms, at the level just above the armoured deck [S183].

### Ladders and hatches

The deck plans mark each ladder head with UP or DN beside a ladder symbol, and each opening as hatch, hatchway, scuttle or manhole, with "over" where the opening is in the deck above [S186] [S187] [S189]. Watertight qualities are printed on the opening: the main deck plate distinguishes flush watertight hatch, round watertight hatch, round watertight scuttle and watertight hatch [S186]. The first platform plate marks hatches over in the second deck where a trunk passes a level without an opening at that level [S190]. Hatch numbers appear where the plan has room, for example forward watertight hatch H-5-157 on the second platform and round watertight hatch 1-207 on the main deck aft [S191] [S186].

### Trunks

Vertical circulation between decks outside the ladders is by numbered trunks, lettered T for trunks, C where a trunk is a control space, and E where it serves machinery. The bow group runs watertight access trunk A-205T on the second deck, watertight trunk A-303 1/2 T and non-tight access trunk A-305A on the third deck, and watertight access trunk A-3TE and watertight trunk A-603T on the first platform [S187] [S189] [S190]. ## 7. What differs between the WWII arrangement and the 1984 sheets

This section is short because the only dated pre-1984 general arrangement drawing reached in this session is the 1954 sheet. What it supports is set out first; what could not be established is in gaps-05.md.

**The compartment numbering itself barely changed.** The 1954 inboard profile letters the after end of the ship with the same numbers the 1984 first platform plate uses: steering gear room C-436E, steering gear power and control room C-432E, refrigeration machinery C-428E, meats C-422A and C-420A, fruit and vegetables C-414A, provision storerooms C-515A, C-524A and C-527A, laundry C-339EA, laundry storerooms C-343A and C-344A, and the crew's quarters C-326L, C-329L and C-332L [S193] [S190] [S189]. A level designer can therefore treat the compartment grid as stable across the ship's life and treat what fills each compartment as the thing that changed.

**Aviation fuel became jet fuel.** On the 1954 profile the stern first-platform compartment is lettered C-439 GAS, a gasoline compartment under the manual's usage letters [S193] [S181]. On the 1984 first platform plate the same position holds JP-5 stowage tank C-439-1J, JP-5 pump room C-439-2J and JP-5 overflow C-439-3J, with a JP-5 fueling station on the main deck above [S190] [S186].

**The after main deck became a helicopter deck.** On the 1954 profile compartment C-101E sits on the main deck aft [S193]. On the 1984 main deck plate that compartment is the helicopter service area C-101E, with a helicopter deck, helicopter ramps P/S and helicopter safety nets P/S abaft it [S186].

**Spaces that exist only because of the 1980s weapon fit.** The following carry 1984 systems and should be subtracted from any reconstruction of the wartime ship: Tomahawk computer room A-217-2C on the second deck [S187]; Tomahawk UPS room B-0117C and Tomahawk hydraulic power rooms nos. 1 to 4 on the 01 level [S185]; Harpoon equipment rooms nos. 1 and 2 on the 01 level [S185]; armoured box launchers nos. 1 to 8 and Harpoon launchers nos. 1 to 4 on the 03 level [S184]; CIWS magazines nos. 1 to 4, CIWS control room no. 2 and the CIWS equipment storeroom and workshop no. 2 on the 04 level [S184]; CIWS control room no. 1, CIWS mounts nos. 21 and 22, Mk 137 launchers and SRBOC platforms on the 05 level [S184]; CIWS workshop B-0601E on the 06 level [S184]; AN/SPS-49 equipment and cooling rooms on the 05 level, AN/SPS-67 room, AN/SPS-49(V) motor generator room, TACAN and radar switchboard room and IFF and radar repair room on the 07 level [S184]; electronic warfare room and cooling room on the 010 level [S184]; Nixie equipment room C-218E on the second deck [S187].

**Habitability and pollution-control spaces that are late additions.** The collecting, holding and transfer system appears at several levels: CHT receiving and transfer tank B-4-10W on the first platform, CHT pump rooms nos. 2 and 3 with their tanks aft, and CHT pump room no. 1 A-609E with tank A-608 1/2 W in the hold forward [S190] [S192]. A laundry water reclamation tank C-438 1/2 W sits on the first platform aft [S190]. The second deck carries a ladies' retiring room A-105L [S186]. AFFF stations and an AFFF storage tank appear on the second deck, third deck and first platform [S187] [S189] [S190].

**What the 1984 plates do not show at all**, and which a wartime reconstruction has to restore from other sources: the aircraft catapults and the aircraft crane on the fantail, the aviation gasoline system that served them, the 40 mm and 20 mm gun galleries, and the four 5" twin mounts removed to make room for the armoured box launchers. None of these is drawn or lettered on any plate read here; the 1984 main deck plate shows the helicopter deck in that space and the 01 level carries a 40 mm magazine B-0104M as the only surviving 40 mm space [S186] [S185] [S183]. The removals themselves are recorded in gaps-05.md because no fetched source states them.

## Conflicts between sources

The transverse bulkhead near frame 35 is called out at frame 35 on the second deck plate and at frame 36 on the third deck and first platform plates; the bulkhead near frame 135 is called out at frame 136 on the second deck plate and frame 135 on the third deck and first platform plates. Both pairs are recorded as the plates print them and are not reconciled [S187] [S189] [S190].

The main battery plot and the secondary battery plot forward are both lettered A-424C on the first platform plate, so the two plotting rooms share one compartment number [S190]. The aft plotting rooms are lettered separately, main battery plotting room aft C-303C and secondary battery plotting room aft B-385C [S189].

No conflict was found between the 1954 and 1984 documents on deck naming or on the compartment numbering scheme [S193] [S181].
