# Impero

Fourth ship of the class, never completed. Built by Ansaldo at Genoa-Sestri Ponente, laid down 14 May 1938, launched 15 November 1939 [S28, p. 169; S20].

The Marina Militare records the launch at Genoa on 15 November 1939 and the striking in 1947 [S10].

## Why she was built and why she was not finished

She and Roma were ordered because the Naval Staff judged two 35,000-ton battleships, even with four rebuilt older ships, too few against a possible combination of French and British forces in the Mediterranean; the two new ships differed only slightly from the first pair [S19]. The programme context, the naval programmes of the later 1930s and the decision to lay down a second pair, is set out in the Italian Navy's own archive bulletin [S16].

After Italy entered the war the incomplete ship was moved from Genoa, out of fear of French attack, and work was not resumed [S28, p. 178]. The Marina Militare states that she was transferred after launching from Genoa to Trieste, and that by the armistice of 8 September 1943 her state of completion was so backward that she counted as an unusable hull [S10].

## Characteristics

No figures were ever measured for a completed ship. The design figures carried on the Marina Militare data sheet are those of Roma: length 240.7 m overall and 227.5 m between perpendiculars, beam 32.9 m, draft 10.5 m, normal displacement 44,050 t and full load 46,215 t, 8 boilers, 4 turbines, 4 screws, 140,000 HP, 30 knots, 4,000 t of fuel oil and 3,920 miles of endurance [S10]. Because she was not completed, her final displacement is unknown [S29, pp. 289-290].

The same sheet lists an armament of ten 381/50 guns, which conflicts with the nine in three triple turrets that every other source gives for the class [S10; S28, p. 169; S22].

## Fate

Taken over incomplete by German forces on 10 September 1943 and used as a target [S22; S28, p. 178]. Sunk by American bombing on 20 February 1945 at Trieste [S22; S28, pp. 175-178]. Raised in October 1947, towed to Venice and broken up [S28, pp. 175-178]; the Italian class sheet gives her striking as 27 March 1947 [S20] and the Marina Militare as 1947 [S10].
