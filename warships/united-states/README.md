# United States Navy battleships, 1939 to 1945

In September 1939 the US Navy had 15 battleships in commission, all completed between 1912 and 1923: Arkansas of the Wyoming class and the two New York, two Nevada, two Pennsylvania, three New Mexico, two Tennessee and three Colorado class ships [R1, R3, R4, R5, R6, R7, R8]. Wyoming had served as a gunnery training ship since 1931 [R1, R13] and Utah as a radio-controlled target ship since 1932 [R2, R14]. Ten fast battleships joined the fleet during the war: North Carolina and Washington in 1941 [R9], the four South Dakota class ships in 1942 [R10], and the four Iowa class ships in 1943 and 1944 [R11]. Japanese carrier aircraft sank Arizona and Oklahoma at Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941 [R5, R4]; Arizona was struck from the register on 1 December 1942 [R21], and Oklahoma, salvaged on 6 November 1943 [R4], was sold in 1946 and sank under tow in 1947 [R19]. Utah was sunk in the same attack [R2, R14]. West Virginia, California and Nevada were sunk or run aground at Pearl Harbor, salvaged, and returned to service [R8, R7, R4]. The fate column below records no other loss to enemy action [R1 to R11].

## Conflicts between sources

- New York sinking date. The DANFS body text says she went down on 7 July 1948; the photo caption on the same page and navypedia both give 8 July 1948 [R16, R3].
- Wyoming's end. navypedia gives "stricken 9.1947" [R1]; DANFS gives decommissioning on 1 August 1947 and no strike date [R13].
- South Dakota's fate. The NHHC DANFS entry fetched for South Dakota carries no disposal sentence; the strike date June 1962 rests on navypedia alone [R10].
- Iowa and New Jersey. The NHHC DANFS entries end with the 1958 and 1957 decommissionings [R35, R36]; the 2006 and 1999 strike dates rest on navypedia [R11].
