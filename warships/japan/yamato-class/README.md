# Yamato class (Imperial Japanese Navy)

The largest battleships ever built. Two were completed as battleships, Yamato and Musashi; the third hull, Shinano, was completed as an aircraft carrier; the fourth, Hull No. 111, was broken up incomplete. The Technical Mission's own summary calls the class a design of 1934 to 1937 displacing some 73,000 tons fully loaded and carrying a 46 cm main battery [S12, p. 1].

## The evidence problem

This dossier rests more heavily on post-war interrogation than any other in the repo. The Japanese destroyed most design records in August 1945, so the US Naval Technical Mission to Japan reports of 1945 and 1946 are the tier 1 source for the hull, armour, machinery and guns, and they record what surviving officers and arsenal staff told the mission rather than what a drawing showed. Several reports were read page by page as images because the scans carry no reliable text layer. Where the mission's figures disagree with the secondary compilations, both sit side by side in the facet files.

One disagreement is worth naming here. The mission's hull design report prints Yamato's main turret face as 20 inches of Vickers cemented armour and never states the 650 mm figure that secondary sources repeat; the armour facet records both and says which document each comes from [S11, p. 17; S12, p. 17].

## What makes the class distinct

- A 46 cm/45 gun, the largest ever mounted at sea, officially designated the 40 cm Type 94 to hide its calibre, a secret held to the end of the war [S5; S11, p. 9].
- A 410 mm main side belt and turret armour heavier than any other battleship carried [S12, p. 17; S28].
- The Type 3 incendiary shrapnel round, fired from the main battery against aircraft. Yamato used it against carrier strikes on 24 October 1944 and again on 7 April 1945 [S1; S24].
- Twin rudders on the centreline, the after one twice the area of the forward one, separated longitudinally so that losing one would not cost steering control [S12].
- A bulbous bow, which the designers credited with cutting resistance by 5 to 6 per cent [S12].
