# Littorio class (Regia Marina)

Fast battleships of the Regia Marina, four planned and three completed: Littorio, renamed Italia in July 1943, Vittorio Veneto, Roma, and the uncompleted Impero. They were Italy's answer to the French Dunkerque class, nominally 35,000 long tons under the Washington treaty and over 40,000 in fact, armed with nine 381 mm guns and good for 30 knots [S28, p. 169; S30, p. 23].

## Design lineage

The class descends from a decade of abandoned studies: a 23,000-ton ship with six 381 mm guns in twin turrets in 1928, a 35,000-ton ship with six 406 mm guns later the same year, an 18,000-ton answer to the Deutschland class in late 1932, and a 26,500-ton design with eight 343 mm guns. The last was dropped for a 35,000-ton ship with 406 mm guns, and the 406 mm gun was itself dropped because no design existed for it and drawing one would have delayed construction, while a 381 mm gun had already been designed for the cancelled Francesco Caracciolo class [S26, pp. 372-379; S28, pp. 169-170; S30, p. 22].

Design work on the class began in 1934, after the French laid down Dunkerque and Strasbourg. Hull forms had been selected by early 1934 and a 37,000 t model was tested in the tanks of the La Spezia arsenal; the final characteristics were approved by the Navy's ship design committee on 12 July 1935, and the first two ships were ordered that October [S26, p. 379].

## What makes the class distinct

- A 381 mm/50 gun of very high muzzle velocity, giving the longest range of any battleship gun then at sea and paying for it in dispersion and a barrel life of 110 to 130 rounds [S1].
- A composite belt in which a 70 mm outer plate strips the cap from an incoming shell before it meets the 280 mm main plate, with the 250 mm gap between them filled with a cement foam [S25, p. 35].
- The Pugliese underwater protection system, an air-filled cylinder 3.8 m across lying in a liquid-filled void along the citadel, designed to collapse and absorb the energy of a torpedo burst [S29, p. 290; S19].
- Anti-aircraft mountings and directors stabilised in four axes, including stabilised platforms under the 90 mm gun crews and stabilised anti-aircraft lookout stations [S3; S19].
- Short legs: under 4,000 miles at 20 knots, which fitted the class for the Mediterranean and nothing wider [S20].
