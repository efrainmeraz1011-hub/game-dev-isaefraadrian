# Littorio class: compartments and deck plans

Status: partial (see gaps.md)

No booklet of general plans or compartment list for this class was found in any reachable archive. What follows is what tier 1 and tier 2 sources state about the arrangement of spaces. The sub-facets a general plans booklet would answer, deck naming and numbering, bulkhead count and compartment numbering, are in gaps.md.

The main armour deck ran the whole length, thinning to 60 mm on 10 mm plating forward and 36 mm on 8 mm plating aft [S25, p. 34].

## Principal spaces

Machinery. Eight boilers amidships, each in its own compartment. Four engine sets: two beneath the conning tower driving the outer shafts, two beneath the after mast driving the inner shafts [S19].

Main battery. For each turret, a shell room below a magazine, both below the gunhouse inside the turret structure [S27, p. 321]. In the turret stalk, a lower and an upper ring carousel at the lowest levels; a ring conveyor on rails in the shell room; a working chamber directly below the gunhouse [S1].

Secondary battery. For each 152 mm turret a handling room below the gunhouse, fed by lower endless chain hoists, with upper endless bucket hoists running into the gunhouse [S2].

Heavy anti-aircraft battery. Magazine, then an electric hoist to the main deck, then a handling room forming the lower compartment of each 90 mm mounting, holding 90 ready rounds [S3].

Armoured citadel. Closed forward of the No. 1 turret magazine and abaft the No. 3 turret magazine by transverse armoured bulkheads [S19].

Damage control. The centrale di galleggiamento was an armoured compartment below the conning tower from which, in action or under air attack, the executive officer directed the fire parties and the balancing of the ship by flooding compartments on the side opposite a hole [S19].

Steering gear. Outside the armoured citadel, protected by its own 100 mm deck below the main deck, and by 100 mm plating and a 200 mm bulkhead abaft the citadel that also covered the shafts and the after diesel generator groups [S19; S25, p. 34].

## Superstructure levels

The conning tower followed the pattern settled on the third group of the Condottieri class. The command bridge and, immediately above it, the admiral's bridge were circular platforms built around the outside of the conning tower, covered forward and fitted with seven rectangular windows, with a plain rail aft and to the sides and two short wings. The two 90 mm anti-aircraft director turrets stood to port and starboard of the command bridge and so close to it that a man could step over the rail onto their roofs [S19].

The taller fore mast was joined to the conning tower by four bridges, one of which served as the signal station. The shorter after mast rose from a structure abaft the funnels that housed the after command position and the searchlights. Two large funnels stood close together amidships, the forward one carrying the small machine-gun control bridge as an outgrowth. A trainable catapult was fitted right aft [S19].

Inside the conning tower an armoured tube 200 mm thick ran from the damage-control central up to the admiral's bridge, carrying both the men moving between levels and the cable runs [S19; S25, p. 61].

## What the wreck survey identified

The Marina Militare minehunter Vieste surveyed the wreck of Roma in July and August 2018 with a tethered autonomous vehicle. Several sections of the wreck were found, capsized and in places buried in sand, inside the Castelsardo canyon. The survey identified the stern section; the bow section, where one of the Pugliese cylinders is visible; and amidships the command bridge, the steering position, the signal station, the turrets holding the medium and heavy calibre guns, a carriage of the after catapult for launching the Ro.43 reconnaissance seaplanes, an anti-aircraft battery of six guns, the rangefinders, and the admiral's bridge from which Admiral Bergamini gave his last orders on 9 September 1943 [S12].

## Conflicts between sources

Horizontal protection is described as three armoured decks, battery, upper and forecastle [S19], and as a weather deck plus a main armour deck [S25, p. 34]. The thickness given for the middle deck differs between accounts: the Italian technical sheet gives 12 mm for the coperta [S19].
