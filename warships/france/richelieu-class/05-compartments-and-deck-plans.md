# Richelieu class: compartments and deck plans

Status: partial (see gaps.md)

No official general arrangement plan of the class was found online. What follows comes from the armour description, the machinery description, the damage narratives, and a Commons drawing that its author states conforms to the drawings in Jordan and Dumas [S42].

## Deck naming

French sources name the decks from the top: the premier pont (upper deck, 24 mm), the pont blindé supérieur or P.B.S. (main armoured deck, 150 to 170 mm) and the pont blindé inférieur or P.B.I. (lower armoured deck, 40 mm with 50 mm slopes, extending forward to frame 233) [S12, p. 11]. The forecastle deck runs long and steps down to main deck level toward the stern [S10, pp. 98-101, 120; S11, p. 260]. Frames are numbered from aft in the French manner in the one instance found: turret VII stands at frame 68.85 and turrets V and VI at frame 54.45 [S14, p. 23; S12, p. 27].

Compartment numbering system and watertight bulkhead count: no source found; see gaps.md.

## Machinery spaces, forward to aft

Boiler room No. 1 (rue de chauffe no 1) lies under the forward tower with boilers 10, 11 and 12 numbered starboard to port; then the forward turbine room driving the outer shafts; then boiler room No. 2 under the funnel with boilers 20, 21 and 22; then the aft turbine room driving the inner shafts. An 18 mm bulkhead separates the forward turbine room from boiler room No. 2 [S10, pp. 117-120]. The three-abreast boiler layout cut 5 m from the machinery length compared with Dunkerque's three rooms of two [S10, pp. 117-120; S10, pp. 97-98]. The Commons power-plant diagram shows the boilers, turbines and turbo generators in this order [S43].

## Electrical and auxiliary spaces

Two turbo generators sit in engine room No. 1 and two in their own compartment aft of the propulsion machinery; the three main diesel generators sit in a compartment between the main battery magazines [S10, pp. 99, 119-120]. Jean Bart's turbo generator room was flooded when she settled by the stern on 10 November 1942 [S10, pp. 159, 161]. The steering compartment holds the direct rudder control and the manual gear for twenty-four men; a secondary conning position is in the superfiring turret [S10, pp. 99-101, 118-119]. Jean Bart's manual steering compartment flooded after the first bomb hit on 8 November 1942, and a shell that burst in the ballast compartment under the quarterdeck flooded the steering compartment again [S10, pp. 156-158].

## Magazines and shell rooms

Each 380 mm turret has two shell rooms and two powder magazines, each pair on one deck at opposite ends of the barbette, the pairs stacked on two decks, each pair feeding one half turret through a handling room below the turret [S1]. The 152 mm shell rooms and magazines lie on one level and share a handling room with stowage for seven complete rounds; Richelieu's amidships turrets, never fitted, would have been 28 m from their magazines [S2]. The main armoured deck thickens to 170 mm over the 380 mm magazines [S12, p. 11], and the torpedo bulkhead thickens to 50 mm abreast the magazines [S20, p. 436; S19, p. 75]. On Clemenceau the existing 152 mm magazines were to be adapted to 100 mm and 37 mm ammunition to avoid redesign [S10, pp. 167, 171]. The rear magazines for the 152 mm and 37 mm guns were flooded by the 1940 torpedo hit and could only be drained once the hole was patched [S10, pp. 138-139]. On Jean Bart a 406 mm shell on 8 November 1942 passed through both armoured decks and burst in the empty magazines of the missing 152 mm turrets [S10, pp. 156-158].

## Hull spaces named in damage reports

The torpedo of 8 July 1940 opened a 9.3 by 8.5 m hole aft between the propeller shafts; water kept entering through the cable tunnels [S10, p. 127; S10, pp. 128, 137]. Marzin's report faulted insufficient pumping equipment, poor welding of bulkheads and turret trunks that were not watertight [S10, pp. 128, 137]. Jean Bart's double bottom was sealed in the rush of June 1940 [S10, pp. 152-154]. At New York in 1943 the concrete poured into Richelieu's damaged stern at Dakar was broken out, deformed bulkheads and plating cut away and the hull sandblasted; the lowest row of portholes was plated over [S10, p. 182].

## Superstructure

A single tower mast stands directly behind the armoured conning tower, with a small deck house aft of the funnel; the superstructure was kept minimal because the short machinery spaces allowed it [S10, pp. 98-101, 120; S11, p. 260]. The tower levels named in the fire control description are the navigating bridge at level 3 (also called the admiral's bridge at "étage 3"), platform 6 for distant lookout and platform 8 for high lookout [S12, pp. 31-33]. After 1943 most command spaces in the tower were converted to other uses [S10, pp. 186, 188-189]. The quarterdeck aft held the hangar, two catapults and the crane until January 1943; the hangar top later carried nine 20 mm guns and the catapult positions four 40 mm quads [S10, pp. 99-101, 120-121; S10, pp. 186-188].
