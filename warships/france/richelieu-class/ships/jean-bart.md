## What differed from the class baseline

### She left the builders unfinished

Work on the propulsion plant was accelerated in May and June 1940 with about 3,500 men. In a month three boilers, two sets of turbines, two turbo generators and two pumps went in, with the internal communications that mattered. The forward main battery turret had its guns and armour but no shell handling gear and no fire control. The superfiring turret had neither guns nor armour, and no secondary turret had been installed [S10, pp. 152-154].

### Concrete in place of armour

At Casablanca, Ronarc'h had the open barbettes of the secondary turrets and of the incomplete superfiring main turret covered with concrete to protect the interior, and much of the hull sealed [S10, pp. 71-72, 154-155].

### Fire control improvised twice

A director was fabricated in 1941 on a torpedo tube platform taken from the destroyer Enseigne Gabolde, carrying a 14 m rangefinder. It worked badly, so from 1942 the main battery was directed by triangulation from observers aboard and at two shore points, Dar Bouazza and Sidi Abderrahman [S10, pp. 71-72, 154-155] [S10, pp. 155-156].

### Armament in November 1942

Jordan and Dumas give the outfit in early November 1942 as one operational main battery turret and an anti-aircraft battery of five twin 90 mm mounts, five 37 mm guns (two in twin mounts and one single), eighteen 13.2 mm guns (two twin mounts and fourteen single) and one 8 mm machine gun. A sixth twin 90 mm mount was planned [S10, pp. 155-156]. NavWeaps states that she had only her turret I guns installed when France fell, and that of the four guns meant for turret II, two were on the dockside at Saint-Nazaire as France collapsed [S1]. The first pair of 90 mm twin mounts had come from the net layer Gladiateur on 18 June 1940 [S10, pp. 152-154]. She had no secondary battery at all: the 152 mm magazines were empty, which is why the 406 mm shell that reached them at 07:25 on 8 November 1942 did not destroy her [S10, p. 158] [S10, pp. 158-159, 161].

### Completed after the war to a different anti-aircraft outfit

She was finished on Richelieu's general lines but with a heavier anti-aircraft battery [S10, pp. 209-210]. Jane's Fighting Ships 1960-1961 gives that battery as twenty-four 100 mm guns in twin mounts and twenty-eight 57 mm Bofors [S50, pp. 150-151]. As completed she carried DRBV 11 air and surface search, DRBV 20 air search and DRBV 30 navigational radar, with DRBC 10A for the main battery, six ACAE sets for the heavy anti-aircraft guns and five DRBC 30B sets for the light guns. Normal displacement had risen to 43,052 t (42,372 long tons) and full load to 49,196 t (48,419 long tons), with draft at 10.9 m (as completed, 1955) [S10, pp. 213, 216-217].

### Her guns went to her sister

Jean Bart's turret I guns were taken to New York in 1943 to replace Richelieu's three ruined barrels [S1] [S10, pp. 186-187]. Both French proposals to complete her in the United States therefore assumed rearmament with 340 mm guns off the old battleship Lorraine [S10, pp. 161-162].
