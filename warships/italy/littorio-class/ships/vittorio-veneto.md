# Vittorio Veneto

Built by Cantieri Riuniti dell'Adriatico at Trieste, laid down 28 October 1934 the same day as Littorio, launched 25 July 1937, delivered incomplete on 28 April 1940 and completed on 15 May 1940 [S25, p. 46; S28, pp. 169, 172; S20].

The class is sometimes named after her rather than Littorio, because the two were laid down on the same date [S28, p. 169; S20]. The name commemorates the battle of Vittorio Veneto [S28, p. 171].

## Ways in which she differed

Her 152 mm battery was the OTO Model 1936 in four triple mountings, against Littorio's Ansaldo Model 1934 [S22; S2].

She went to sea for machinery trials while still incomplete on 23 October 1939, was delivered at Trieste on 28 April 1940, sailed from Trieste on 1 May for final fitting out at La Spezia, and after completion on 15 May 1940 went to Taranto to join the fleet [S28, pp. 169, 172].

On trials she made 133,771 shp and 31.4 knots at light displacement [S29, pp. 289-290].

From 1942 she is credited with thirty-two 20 mm guns, more than either sister at that date [S20].

## Service record in figures

56 missions and 17,828 miles between 10 June 1940 and 8 September 1943, then 3 missions and 2,397 miles to 25 April 1945 [S14, table 1]. The Marina Militare's summary gives 56 war missions, of which 11 were searches for the enemy [S8].

## Radar

An EC-3/ter set was fitted in June 1943 [S22].

## Fate

She sailed with the fleet on 9 September 1943 in the 9th Division with Roma and Italia and was not hit [S13, note 18]. Interned at Malta, then Alexandria, then the Great Bitter Lake [S28, pp. 175-178]. A proposal to use her in the co-belligerency of 1943 to 1945, in support of amphibious operations in southern France and the Pacific, was dropped for political and operational reasons [S8]. Allocated to Britain under the peace treaty, she was moved to La Spezia on 14 October 1946, paid off on 3 January 1948 and broken up [S28, pp. 175-178]; the Marina Militare gives her striking as 1948 and her scrapping at La Spezia [S8; S20].
