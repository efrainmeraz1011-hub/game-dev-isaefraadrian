# Roma

Built by Cantieri Riuniti dell'Adriatico at Trieste, laid down 18 September 1938, launched 9 June 1940, commissioned 14 June 1942, sunk 9 September 1943 [S26, pp. 404, 428; S28, p. 169].

Her construction, with Impero's, was decided because two modern battleships plus the rebuilt older ships were judged too few against a possible combination of the French and British Mediterranean fleets [S19; S16].

## Ways in which she differed

Laid down nearly four years after the first pair, she took several changes. The bow was redesigned to give more freeboard, and was altered again partway through building, on the experience of Vittorio Veneto, to give a finer waterline ending. She was completed with more 20 mm guns than her sisters [S28, pp. 171-172; S25, pp. 418-419].

Length overall 240.68 m against 237.76 m for the first pair, with the same beam and draft [S29, pp. 289-290]. Standard displacement 40,992 t and full load 45,485 t [S29, pp. 289-290]. The Marina Militare's own technical block gives her 240.7 m overall, 32.9 m beam, 10.5 m draft, 44,050 t normal and 46,215 t at full load [S11].

Her 381 mm guns were of the Model 1939 batch; Ansaldo built three of them and OTO six, and OTO built two of her three mountings [S1].

She carried twelve stabilised anti-aircraft lookout stations, six a side, below the 90 mm director turrets [S19].

An EC-3/ter Gufo radar was fitted in August 1943 [S22], and the Gufo is listed as her sensor fit [S20].

## Service

She arrived at Taranto on 21 August 1942 and joined the Ninth Naval Division. She went on no combat mission, the Navy being short of fuel, and moved between Taranto, Naples and La Spezia, where she became fleet flagship [S26, pp. 392, 404]. In fifteen months she made 20 sorties, almost all transfers between bases, covering 2,492 miles and burning 3,320 tonnes of fuel oil in 133 hours under way [S26, p. 410]. The Italian official statistics credit her with 17 missions and 2,523 miles [S14, table 1].

| 23 to 24 June 1943 | two bombs | one aft and to starboard of the after main turret, wrecking staterooms that flooded from broken piping; the second on the roof of the after turret itself, with little damage because of the armour there; no serious damage or flooding, but she sailed to Genoa for repairs, arriving 1 July and returning to La Spezia on 13 August | [S26, p. 404] |

The Marina Militare gives the same repair period, stating that the damage from the American raid kept her out of service until 13 August 1943 [S12].

## Loss, 9 September 1943

She sailed from La Spezia on 9 September 1943 under Captain Adone Del Cima, as flagship of Admiral Carlo Bergamini [S26, p. 405; S13].

|---|---|---|

| seconds later | the magazine of turret number two detonates and blows the turret over the side | [S26, p. 407; S33, pp. 8-34] |
| after that | catastrophic flooding forward, the ship going down by the bow and listing further to starboard, capsizing and breaking in two | [S26, p. 407; S33, pp. 8-34] |

The attackers were eight aircraft of III/KG 100 flying from Istres in France, and the weapons two FX 1400 radio-controlled bombs [S13].

Casualties: the official inquest held after the sinking gives a crew of 1,849 on sailing, 596 survivors and 1,253 lost [S26, p. 407]. Later research, citing Pier Paolo Bergamini, adds about two hundred men of the admiral's staff left out of the inquest, giving 2,021 aboard and 1,393 lost [S36, pp. 529-530]. The Italian Navy's own account gives 1,393 of 2,021 [S13], and the Marina Militare press release gives 1,393 dead and the sinking as taking 28 minutes [S12].

Rear Admiral Stanislao Caraciotti was also killed [S26, p. 407].

## The wreck

The Marina Militare records the wreck as located in 2012 after decades of searching, at more than 1,200 m and about 16 miles off the coast of the Gulf of Asinara in northern Sardinia [S12].

In July and August 2018 the minehunter Vieste re-surveyed it with a tethered autonomous vehicle working to depths over 1,500 m. Several sections were found, capsized and in places buried in sand, in the Castelsardo canyon on rocky ground. The survey imaged the stern section, the bow section with one of the Pugliese cylinders visible, and amidships the command bridge, the steering position, the signal station, the turrets of the medium and heavy guns, a carriage of the after catapult used to launch the Ro.43 seaplanes, an anti-aircraft battery of six guns, the rangefinders and the admiral's bridge [S12].

The hull is a war grave and holds the remains of 1,393 sailors; by decree of 27 May 1949 the date of the sinking became the Day of Remembrance for Sailors Lost at Sea, marked each year at Brindisi and at Porto Torres [S12].
