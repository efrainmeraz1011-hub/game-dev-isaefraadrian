# King George V class battleships (United Kingdom)

Type: battleship. Nation: United Kingdom, Royal Navy. Five ships, completed between December 1940 and August 1942, and the only British battleships designed and built after the inter-war treaty holiday to see war service [S23]. Four survived the war; Prince of Wales was lost to Japanese air attack off Malaya on 10 December 1941 [S2], [S24].

## Design lineage and what makes the class distinct

The class was designed to the 14-inch gun limit Britain was pressing for in the naval treaty talks of the mid-1930s, and the guns had to be ordered in mid-1936 before those talks concluded, because gun and mounting production set the critical path for the ship [S69]. The first arrangement carried twelve guns in three quadruple mountings; to pay for thicker armour around the magazines within the 35,000-ton limit, "B" mounting was cut to two guns, leaving ten in two quadruple Mark III mountings and one twin Mark II [S69], [S23].

That produced the class's two defining features. The first is a main battery of ten 14-inch/45 Mark VII guns in mountings of over 3,000 working parts, with the most elaborate flash-tight arrangements in any British battleship and a record of mechanical failure in action to match: five of ten guns out by the end of King George V's engagement with Bismarck, 241 missed firing opportunities in Duke of York's two hours against Scharnhorst [S6], [S7]. The second is a fully dual-purpose secondary battery, sixteen 5.25-inch guns in eight twin high-angle and low-angle mountings, the first in a British battleship, whose cramped gunhouse and 10-degree-per-second training defeated its own purpose against fast aircraft [S8], [S69].

The class is also the only one whose side protection system was tested in action and then surveyed on the seabed. The system was validated before the war by the Job 74 caisson trials against charges of up to 1,000 pounds (454 kilograms) of TNT; the 2007 expedition to the wreck of Prince of Wales took its name from those trials and concluded that the system had failed its first real test [S24], [S25].
