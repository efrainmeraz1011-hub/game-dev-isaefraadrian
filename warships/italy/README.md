# Regia Marina battleships, 1939 to 1945

In September 1939 the Regia Marina had two battleships in service, Conte di Cavour and Giulio Cesare, whose reconstruction had been completed between 1933 and 1937 [R4]; their half-sisters Caio Duilio and Andrea Doria were still in the yards under a reconstruction that ran from 1937 to 1940 [R4]. Four new Littorio class ships had been ordered, Littorio and Vittorio Veneto around October 1934 and Roma and Impero in 1938 [R4]. Three were completed during the war: Vittorio Veneto on 28 April 1940, Littorio on 6 May 1940, and Roma on 14 June 1942 [R1]. Impero was never completed and was captured incomplete on 10 September 1943 [R1, R4]. Two ships were lost. Conte di Cavour was sunk in shallow water at Taranto by an air-dropped torpedo on 12 November 1940, raised on 1 July 1941, never returned to service, scuttled by her crew on 10 September 1943, raised again by the Germans, and sunk by American aircraft on 15 February 1945 [R3]. Roma was hit by two FX-1400 guided bombs off Sardinia on 9 September 1943 and capsized at 1612 with 1,253 men lost [R5]. Giulio Cesare was handed to the Soviet Union and renamed Novorossiysk, and was lost at Sevastopol on 29 October 1955 [R3]. The other four ships were stricken between 1947 and 1956 [R1, R2].

## Classes in commission, completed, or under construction, September 1939 to September 1945

The class names differ between the two sources. navypedia files the 1940 ships as the Vittorio Veneto class, the 1915 to 1916 ships as the Caio Duilio class, and the 1914 to 1915 ships as the Giulio Cesare class [R1, R2, R3]; regiamarina.net calls them the Littorio or Vittorio Veneto class, the Caio Duilio class, and the Conte di Cavour class [R4]. Neither source prints pennant numbers for these ships. Dates in the fate column are as the cited source gives them.

## Conflicts between sources

- The date of the Taranto raid. The navypedia Vittorio Veneto class page dates the British carrier raid that hit Littorio to 11 November 1940 [R1]; the navypedia Caio Duilio and Giulio Cesare class pages date the same raid to 12 November 1940 [R2, R3].
- Littorio's renaming. The navypedia ships table gives "Littorio, 7.1943- Italia" [R1]; the same page's naval service text gives 30 July 1943 [R1]; regiamarina.net gives the renaming as after 25 July 1943 [R5].
- Giulio Cesare's transfer to the Soviet Union. The navypedia ships table gives February 1949 [R3]; the naval service text on the same page gives 1948 [R3].
- Roma's completion. navypedia gives 14 June 1942 [R1]; regiamarina.net gives June 1942 without a day [R4].
