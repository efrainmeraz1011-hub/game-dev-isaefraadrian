# Richelieu class: sensors, fire control and communications

Status: partial (see gaps.md)

## Directors and rangefinders, as designed and completed 1940

Five main directors controlled the armament: three stacked on the foremast and two aft, one for the secondary battery on top of the funnel in a mack arrangement and one for the main battery on a deck house. The foremast main battery director carried a 14 m stereoscopic rangefinder and the aft one an 8 m rangefinder; each main turret had its own 14 m rangefinder for local control. The two secondary directors on the foremast carried 8 m and 6 m rangefinders and the mack director a 6 m rangefinder; each secondary turret had an 8 m rangefinder. Two 3 m rangefinders for the admiral's staff sat on sponsons on the conning tower. The directors passed range and bearing to a central processing station that fed the guns [S10, pp. 101, 107-109].

Dumas and Jordan and Dumas via the French article add the makers and positions: the arrangement repeats Dunkerque's. On the fore tower, from bottom to top, director A for the main battery with a 14 m OPL (Optique de Précision de Levallois-Perret) triplex stereoscopic rangefinder, then director 2 for the secondary battery against surface targets with an 8 m OPL duplex rangefinder, then director 1 on top for secondary anti-aircraft fire with a 6 m OPL rangefinder. On the aft tower, director 3 for the secondary battery with a 6 m rangefinder. The auxiliary main battery director B, with an 8 m OPL duplex rangefinder, stood between the aft tower and turret VII. All directors were gas-tight and lightly armoured against splinters. Each 380 mm turret had a 14 m OPL duplex stereoscopic rangefinder and each 152 mm turret an 8 m OPL duplex. A director with a 3 m OPL rangefinder first placed on the highest platform of the fore tower was moved to the conning tower roof. Two staff directors with 3 m SOM rangefinders on the sides of the admiral's bridge (level 3) were replaced, when the 100 mm battery was fitted, by directors with 4 m OPL rangefinders to control the 100 mm guns, and two more 4 m directors went on the navigating bridge below [S10, pp. 107-109] [S12, pp. 31-33].

Optical lookout: close lookout from the navigating bridge at level 3, distant lookout from platform 6, and high lookout against mines and torpedoes from platform 8. For night action five searchlights sat on the fore tower, one on the front face and two on each side [S10, pp. 107-109] [S12, pp. 31-33].

Fire control equipment for the main and secondary batteries was installed at Brest between 19 and 27 May 1940 [S10, pp. 123-124]. The upper main battery director on the foremast never became operational, and was removed in February 1943 so the ship could pass under the Brooklyn Bridge; it was not replaced [S10, pp. 186, 188-189].

## Damage to the directors, 1940

The torpedo hit of 8 July 1940 knocked two fire control directors off their tracks and bent the starboard shafts [S10, p. 127]. The directors had to be re-seated, wiring damaged by flooding and leaking oil replaced, and generators rebuilt [S10, pp. 137-138]. At Dakar the 152 mm turrets lacked a director able to track aircraft [S10, pp. 125-126].

The US Navy refused to transfer its latest radar to the French in 1943 on security grounds, which limited the refit to anti-aircraft guns, auxiliary equipment and repairs [S10, pp. 125, 181-182, 186]. In February 1945 Richelieu drilled with her anti-aircraft radars and command systems before rejoining the fleet [S10, p. 196].

## Fire control after 1943

The main battery fire control systems were replaced and those for the secondary guns repaired with new wiring and telephones; the Anschütz gyrocompasses were replaced by Sperry models [S10, pp. 186, 188-189]. Each 40 mm quadruple mount had its own Mk 51 director [S10, pp. 186-188]. Most of the command spaces in the tower were converted to other uses [S10, pp. 186, 188-189].

## Jean Bart's improvised fire control, 1941 to 1942

At Casablanca an improvised director was built in 1941 on the torpedo tube platform of the old destroyer Enseigne Gabolde as a base for a 14 m rangefinder; a 3 m rangefinder went on the conning tower for navigation and two more on the bridge wings in October 1941 [S10, pp. 71-72, 154-155]. The director worked poorly, so after the test firing of 19 May 1942 a triangulation system with observers aboard and at Dar Bouazza and Sidi Abderrahman was set up to direct the main battery [S10, pp. 155-156].
