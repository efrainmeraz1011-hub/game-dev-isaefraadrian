# Imperial Japanese Navy battleships, 1939 to 1945

In September 1939 the Imperial Japanese Navy had ten battleships in commission: the four Kongo class ships completed in 1913 to 1915 as battlecruisers [R1], Fuso and Yamashiro (1915 and 1917) [R2], Ise and Hyuga (1917 and 1918) [R3], and Nagato and Mutsu (1920 and 1921) [R4]. Two Yamato class ships joined them, Yamato on 16 December 1941 and Musashi on 5 August 1942 [R5]; the third hull, Shinano, was completed as an aircraft carrier in 1944 and the fourth was broken up on the slip in March 1942 [R5]. Eleven of the twelve battleships were lost between November 1942 and July 1945: Hiei and Kirishima off Guadalcanal in November 1942 [R8, R9], Mutsu by magazine explosion at Hashirajima on 8 June 1943 [R4, R15], Musashi in the Sibuyan Sea on 24 October 1944 [R17], Fuso and Yamashiro in Surigao Strait on 25 October 1944 [R11, R12], Kongo to a submarine on 21 November 1944 [R7], Yamato south of Kyushu on 7 April 1945 [R16], and Hyuga, Ise and Haruna to carrier aircraft at Kure in July 1945 [R14, R13, R10]. Nagato alone survived to the surrender; she was handed to the United States on 20 September 1945 and sunk at the Bikini atomic tests on 29 July 1946 [R4]. The disarmed target ship Settsu, a former battleship, was sunk at Kure on 24 July 1945 [R6].

## Conflicts between sources

- Hyuga's loss date. navypedia gives "sunk 24.7.1945" [R3]; the combinedfleet tabular record has the fatal attack on 24 July, the stern aground on 25 July, and the wreck settling to the bottom on 26 July 1945 [R14].
- Kongo's torpedo hits. navypedia says one to three torpedoes depending on the account [R1]; the combinedfleet record describes the attack by Sealion without a fixed count in the sentences read [R7].
- Mutsu's cause. navypedia records "crew negligence" as the usual explanation [R4]; the combinedfleet record lists several theories, including improperly manufactured Type 3 ammunition and deliberate sabotage, without settling on one [R15].
