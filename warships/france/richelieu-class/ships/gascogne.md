Status: covered

## What differed from the class baseline

Darlan chose the Project B variant for this ship on 19 March 1938, the radical one. The rest of the naval staff preferred it to the variant given to Clemenceau [S10, pp. 166-167].

### One main battery turret aft

The superfiring 380 mm turret moved to the quarterdeck. The three secondary turrets moved to the centreline, two superfiring over the forward main turret and one superfiring aft. This freed the sides of the ship for the anti-aircraft battery, away from main battery blast, and gave all-round fire for both main and secondary guns instead of the wide blind arc astern of the other designs. It also forced the superstructure forward, which meant redesigning the hull to move the machinery spaces and hold the trim [S10, pp. 171-172].

### Aviation

The aft turret took the space the hangar had used. The designers adopted the American solution of an internal hangar in the hull with a crane lifting aircraft to a single catapult. Only three aircraft could be carried, two in the hangar and one on the catapult, and the type was to be the SNCAC NC.420 floatplane rather than the Loire 130 [S10, p. 172].

### Armour and displacement

Moving a turret aft lengthened the belt. To hold displacement, the compartment filled with ébonite mousse was shortened and partly turned into fuel stowage. The secondary turrets were given thicker armour to compensate for having fewer guns, limited by the power of the turret motors. Displacement came down slightly against Clemenceau: 35,000 long tons standard and 44,438 t (43,736 long tons) full load (as planned, 1938) [S10, pp. 173-175].

### Battery as planned

Eight 380 mm, nine 152 mm in three centreline triples, sixteen 100 mm, twelve 37 mm and thirty-six 13.2 mm, with a 320 mm belt (as planned, 1938) [S10, pp. 171-175].

Complement was to be 1,670 officers and men, as for Clemenceau (as planned, 1938) [S10, pp. 173-175].
