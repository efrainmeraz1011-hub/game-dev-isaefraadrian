# Bismarck class

Type: battleship. Nation: Germany, Kriegsmarine. Two ships, Bismarck and Tirpitz.

Bismarck is the documented ship of the pair. Tirpitz appears where a source states her figures, and the differences between the two are collected in `ships/bismarck.md` and `ships/tirpitz.md`.

## Design lineage

Bismarck's building contract went to Blohm & Voss on 16 November 1935 and Tirpitz's on 14 June 1936 [S20; S19]. navypedia dates the approval of the design for battleships "F" and "G" to 16 November 1935, traces the programme to the Anglo-German Naval Agreement of 18 June 1935, and says the ships structurally repeated Scharnhorst and differed chiefly in the main artillery, the 15 cm secondary guns all going into turrets and the 10.5 cm anti-aircraft battery growing; it adds that the declared 35,000 t was about 7,000 t below the real figure [S38, project history]. That source is tier 3 and no tier 1 or 2 source fetched for this dossier states the lineage; treat it accordingly.

Three design changes from the preceding class are visible in the facet files. The longitudinal torpedo bulkheads were carried vertically rather than sloped as in Scharnhorst [S3, underwater protection]. Turret Anton alone received a rotary cartridge-case ejector drum in place of the flap cover used in the other three turrets and in Scharnhorst and Gneisenau, whose flaps had admitted water during the action with Renown on 9 April 1940 [S9, mount data]. The gun models themselves predate the ships: 38 cm SK C/34 designed 1934, 15 cm SK C/28 designed 1928 and in service 1934, 10.5 cm SK C/33 designed 1933 and in service 1935 [S4; S9; S10; S11].

## What distinguishes the class

- Displacement well above the declared figure. Prisoners mostly held to 35,000 tons; one who had been aboard during building explained that the figure excluded fuel, feed water, domestic water and stores and counted half the ammunition, from which the Admiralty concluded that deep displacement was not far short of 50,000 tons [S15, §VII(1)]. The builder's build-up runs from 40,250 metric tons empty to 53,486 metric tons maximum [S1; S2].
- Armour of 19,082 metric tons, about 40% of the 47,870 metric ton designed combat weight, with a 320 mm KC belt covering 70% of the waterline [S1; S3].
- A two-layer horizontal scheme rather than one thick deck: a 50 to 80 mm upper deck above a main armour deck of 80 to 120 mm with slopes running down to the lower edge of the belt [S3, horizontal protection].
- Vertical 45 mm Ww torpedo bulkheads, with the empty counterflooding space outboard of the liquid-loaded compartments. The US Naval Technical Mission judged that arrangement a liability in Tirpitz, because rupturing the outboard voids gave a larger listing moment than an inboard void would have [S3; S18, §3(e)].
- Three shafts, not four: twelve Wagner high-pressure boilers in six rooms feeding three turbine sets, 150,170 hp and 30.12 knots in Bismarck [S5; S1].
- Two separate medium batteries: twelve 15 cm SK C/28 in six twin turrets, and sixteen 10.5 cm SK C/33 in eight twin anti-aircraft mounts [S4].
- A transverse double catapult amidships extending to 48 m, four Arado Ar 196 carried with room for six [S7].
- No torpedo tubes and no mines in Bismarck [S15, §VII(1)]; Tirpitz received two quadruple 53.3 cm mounts in October 1941 [S19].
- A complement above 2,000: 103 officers and 1,962 men as commissioned, more than 2,200 aboard for Rheinübung [S29].
