# HMS Anson (pennant 79)

Ordered as Jellicoe and renamed before launch [S23]. The most heavily modernised ship of the class by 1945, and the only one never to fire her main armament in anger. The timeline is in facet 08.

## Completion and trials

Built by Swan Hunter and Wigham Richardson at Wallsend, laid down 20 July 1937, launched 24 February 1940, completed 22 June 1942 [S23]. On completion she made 27.62 knots on the measured mile [S4].

## Service

Home Fleet from 1942 to 1944, providing distant cover for the Arctic convoys PQ18, JW51B, JW52, JW54A, JW54B and JW58, and cover for the carrier strikes against Tirpitz in April 1944 [S4]. She sailed for the Mediterranean with Duke of York on 25 April 1945 [S4] and took part in the reoccupation of Hong Kong [S4]. The tier 3 monograph records that she was the only ship of the class never to fire her main guns in anger, and that she reached the Pacific too late for action [S69].

Two of the photographic series of the class were taken aboard her at Scapa Flow in October 1942 and are the main visual record of daily life in the class [S32], [S36], [S47].

## Devonport refit, July 1944 to March 1945

Her radar outfit was renewed and her aircraft facilities removed, the space given over to accommodation and boat stowage [S4]. Navypedia lists the March 1945 result as the heaviest electronic fit in the class: seven Type 262, two Type 274, four Type 275, Type 277, Type 281B and Type 293, together with a Type 651 electronic countermeasures suite, with Type 273, Type 281, five Type 282, Type 284 and four Type 285 removed; the close-range battery became two quadruple 40 mm Bofors, two octuple and two quadruple 2-pdr mountings, ten twin and thirteen single Oerlikons, and full displacement rose to 45,360 t [S23].

## The systems unique to her

She was the only battleship in the class, and the only British ship before the end of the war, to receive the complete HACS Mark VI system. That was a redesign of the high-angle director built around the Type 275 radar, which let the radar operator track a new target while the director stayed on the one being engaged, gave a limited blind-fire capability, and had level and cross-level stabilisation with Metadyne power, scooter control and rate aiding in laying and training. The table itself was unchanged from earlier marks and the tachymetric fittings gave trouble. Her Mark VI directors weighed about 12.5 tons (12.7 mt), against about 5.5 tons for a Mark IV with Type 285 [S13].

Late in the war all eight of her 5.25-inch mountings were converted to the RP10 standard, which added remote power control, vertical rollers to steady the mounting, smoothed drives in the training and elevating gear, and a power-operated loading tray with an automatic ramming link. Only the two after mountings of the cruiser Argonaut received the same conversion [S8]. She was also one of at least three British ships fitted with USN Mark 14 gyro sights on quadruple pom-poms in 1945, and by September 1945 carried six Mark VII*P quadruple mountings [S9].

## Conflicts between sources

- Laid-down date. 20 July 1937 [S23]; 22 July 1937 in the tier 3 monograph [S69].
- Quadruple pom-pom mountings in 1945. Six Mark VII*P by September 1945 [S9]; navypedia's March 1945 list gives two quadruple mountings, with two more added later in 1945 [S23].
