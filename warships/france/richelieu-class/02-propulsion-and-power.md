# Richelieu class: propulsion and power

Status: partial (see gaps.md)

## Boilers

Six oil-fired Sural forced-circulation boilers, "Sural" being short for suralimenté, pressure-fired. Indret built Richelieu's; Penhoët and A. C. de la Loire built Jean Bart's. The type was experimental and a risk for the fleet's most powerful ships, but proved reliable in service [S10, pp. 99-101, 117-118]. Each boiler measured 6.90 m long, 4.55 m high and 4.50 m wide, against 5.33 m, 5.34 m and 6.50 m for a Dunkerque boiler; three stood abreast in each boiler room, a combined width of 13.5 m where two Dunkerque boilers took 13 m [S10, pp. 117-120]. Because three fitted abreast, the boiler rooms fell from three to two and the armoured length shrank by almost 5 m [S10, pp. 97-98]. The boilers ducted into a single funnel [S10, pp. 99-101, 117-118], built into the aft tower as a combined mast and funnel [S11, p. 260].

## Turbines, shafts and propellers

Four Parsons geared steam turbines on four shafts [S10, pp. 99-101, 117-118]. The machinery is arranged on the unit system: boiler room No. 1 (boilers 10, 11 and 12, numbered starboard to port) under the forward tower, then the forward turbine room driving the outer shafts, then boiler room No. 2 (boilers 20, 21 and 22) under the funnel, then the aft turbine room driving the inner shafts. An 18 mm armoured bulkhead separates the forward turbine room from boiler room No. 2 [S10, pp. 117-120]. One unit can be lost to damage while the other keeps steaming [S10, pp. 99-101, 117-118]. All four propellers have four blades and a diameter of 4.88 m [S10, pp. 99-101, 117-118] (4.87 m in [S10, pp. 117-120] and [S36]).

## Electrical plant

Four 1,500 kW turbo generators and three 1,000 kW diesel generators, with two 140 kW diesels for emergency power. The turbo generators supply the ship underway; two sit in engine room No. 1 and two in their own compartment aft of the machinery. The main diesels sit in a compartment between the main battery magazines and are used only in port; they can be overloaded to 1,250 kW for five minutes [S10, pp. 99, 119-120]. Voltage: no source found; see gaps.md.

Jean Bart's two turbo generators were installed in the month before her escape in June 1940 [S10, pp. 152-154]; on 10 November 1942 flooding submerged her turbo generator room and bomb shock disabled the main diesels, leaving the backup diesels as her only power [S10, pp. 159, 161].

## Steering gear

A single counter-balanced rudder, controlled from the conning tower, from the secondary conning position in the superfiring turret, or directly in the steering compartment. With power lost, a manual steering gear needing twenty-four men takes over [S10, pp. 99-101, 118-119]. After the 1940 torpedo hit bent the starboard shafts and deformed the hull, Richelieu crossed the Atlantic in 1943 at 14 knots with seven degrees of rudder held on [S10, p. 150].

## Underway replenishment

The Marine nationale began fuelling-at-sea trials on 16 June 1936 between two contre-torpilleurs in the baie de Seine; the battleship Provence tried it with a torpedo boat on 18 November and 5 December 1936 using 80 mm and then 120 mm hoses at 20 tonnes an hour. A note of 6 January 1939 ordered fuelling gear fitted to recent contre-torpilleurs, cruisers and capital ships. An instruction of 2 April 1940 called for an internal pipe leading to the bow on ships able to take it, the astern (en flèche) method then being preferred; the abeam method spread from late 1940 because it needed less work [S39, citing Moulin and Maurand pp. 18-19].

No source states the fittings carried by Richelieu herself. In service she refuelled with the Eastern Fleet: the fleet stopped to refuel on 15 May 1944 during Operation Transom [S10, pp. 192-193], and Force 63 "commenced refueling at sea" before dividing on the night of 10 to 11 April 1945 [S29]. ## Machinery history

Richelieu's engines were unused from July 1940 until trials on 25 to 29 January 1943 [S10, p. 150]. At New York the turbines were repaired, the boilers re-tubed, much of the wiring replaced and a degaussing cable fitted; the Anschütz gyrocompasses gave way to Sperry models [S10, pp. 186, 188-189]. The starboard shaft brackets were straightened and the inboard starboard shaft, too badly bent to keep, was replaced by a Bethlehem Steel forging fitted in June 1943 [S10, p. 182]. On the passage to Suez in March 1944 the boiler blowers gave too little air, fuel burned incompletely, tubes fouled and overheated; repairs at Aden did not cure it [S10, p. 192]. Boiler trouble and hull fouling cut her speed by September 1944 [S10, p. 195]; trials in February 1945 showed the boiler problem finally corrected [S10, p. 196]. The Durban refit of July to August 1945 re-tubed the boilers again [S10, pp. 199-200], and the Cherbourg docking of March to July 1946 replaced the starboard propeller and overhauled the boilers [S10, pp. 200-201].
