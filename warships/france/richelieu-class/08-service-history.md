Sailed from Brest at 04:00 the next morning with the destroyers Fougueux and Frondeur, cruising first at 22 knots and then at 18 after boiler trouble | [S10, pp. 124-125] |

| 23 to 25 September 1940 | Operation Menace. The Free French landing at Rufisque failed on the afternoon of the 23rd; on the 25th, after Resolution was badly damaged, Cunningham ordered the withdrawal | [S49] |

| 8 November 1942 | Operation Torch. Darlan defected to the Allies with the rest of the fleet | [S10, p. 150] |
| 25 to 29 January 1943 | Sea trials off Dakar to test engines unused since July 1940 | [S10, p. 150] |
| 30 January 1943 | Sailed from Dakar with Montcalm at 14 knots, rudder held at seven degrees | [S10, p. 150] [S13, pp. 37, 50] |
| 11 February 1943 | Arrived at New York; docked in Dock No. 5 at the Brooklyn Navy Yard on the 18th | [S10, p. 150] [S13, pp. 37, 50] |
| 29 April 1943 | CV Lambert replaced Deramond during the refit | [S10, pp. 125, 181-182, 186] |
| 14 October 1943 | Left the United States escorted by the destroyers Tarbell and Ellet, holding 24 knots in heavy seas | [S10, p. 190] |
| 24 November 1943 | Fraser inspected her at Scapa Flow. A Type 284 gunnery radar was fitted and Home Fleet training began | [S10, p. 190] |
| 10 to 12 February 1944 | Operation Posthorn with Anson and Furious off Norway | [S10, pp. 190-191] [S16, pp. 307, 313] |
| 24 to 28 March 1944 | Photographed en route east. The NARA caption on 80-G-250275 reads "DATE 24-28 Mar. 1944 ... The SS RICHELIEU, French battleship, enroute to the Pacific" | [S47] |
| 26 March 1944 | Stopped at Algiers for supplies; boiler tube fouling began on the passage to Suez and repairs at Aden did not cure it | [S10, p. 192] |
| 10 April 1944 | Arrived at Trincomalee. The Queen Elizabeth chronology records her arrival from the United Kingdom on 10/4/44 after the New York refit and Home Fleet service from 11/43 to 3/44 | [S10, p. 192] [S29] |
| 16 to 19 April 1944 | Operation Cockpit, the carrier strike on Sabang | [S10, pp. 192-193] [S16, p. 319] |
| 7 to 27 May 1944 | Operation Transom, the strike on Surabaya | [S10, pp. 193-194, 199] [S16, p. 323] |
| 31 May 1944 | CV Merveilleux du Vignaux replaced Lambert in command, at Colombo | [S10, pp. 193-194, 199] [S17, pp. 204-205] |
| 19 to 23 June 1944 | Operation Pedal, the carrier strike on Port Blair | [S10, p. 194] [S16, pp. 334, 344] |
| 22 to 27 July 1944 | Operation Crimson. She bombarded Sabang on 25 July, firing 81 main battery APC between 06:54 and 07:15 | [S10, pp. 194-195] |
| 6 September 1944 | Left Trincomalee for refit, reaching Algiers on 23 September, Toulon on 1 October and Casablanca on 10 October, where new radar was fitted | [S10, p. 195] [S17, p. 217] |
| 23 January 1945 | Left Casablanca for Gibraltar, arriving on the 25th for hull cleaning. Trials in February showed the boiler fault cured | [S10, p. 196] [S17, pp. 195-217] |
| 20 March 1945 | Arrived at Trincomalee, now with the East Indies Fleet | [S10, p. 196] [S17, pp. 195-217] |
| 8 to 20 April 1945 | Operation Sunfish. She bombarded Sabang on 11 April with seven main battery salvos | [S10, p. 197] [S16, p. 408] |
| 27 April to 8 May 1945 | Operation Bishop. Car Nicobar airfields on 29 April with 80 main and 45 secondary rounds, Port Blair the same evening, and 120 secondary rounds into Port Blair harbour on 2 May | [S10, pp. 198-199] [S16, p. 412] |
| 9 to 18 May 1945 | Operation Dukedom. Haguro was sunk by destroyers before Richelieu and Cumberland could reach her | [S10, p. 199] [S16, p. 417] |
| 3 June 1945 | Le Triomphant brought spare gear; Richelieu was sent to Durban for a refit | [S10, pp. 199-200] |
| 18 July to 10 August 1945 | At Durban. Work ran 31 July to 10 August. Thirteen 20 mm guns were landed and four 40 mm fitted in their place | [S10, pp. 199-200] |
| 18 August 1945 | Back at Trincomalee, by which time Japan had surrendered | [S10, pp. 199-200] [S18, pp. 325, 329] |
| 7 September 1945 | Sailed with HMS Nelson for Operation Zipper, the landing on Sumatra. On 9 September she detonated a magnetic mine; hull plates were pushed in 10 to 12 cm and she stayed with the fleet | [S10, pp. 200-201] [S18, pp. 330-334] |
| 11 to 16 September 1945 | Moved to Singapore for Operation Tiderace on the 12th, then returned to Trincomalee on the 16th | [S10, pp. 200-201] [S18, pp. 330-334] |
| 27 September 1945 | Sailed for Indochina escorting the transports Queen Emma and Princess Beatrix, and there served as staging area, hospital, artillery support and troop transport | [S10, pp. 200-201] [S16, pp. 429, 432] |
| 20 to 26 November 1945 | Operation Mapor at Nha Trang, in fire support of troops ashore | [S10, pp. 200-201] |
| 29 December 1945 | Left Indochina, reaching Toulon on 11 February 1946 | [S10, pp. 200-201] |
| 16 March to 20 July 1946 | Dry-docked at Cherbourg: starboard propeller replaced, mine damage corrected, boilers overhauled | [S10, pp. 200-201] |
| 1 April 1949 | Placed in reserve so that funds could go to completing Jean Bart | [S10, pp. 204-205] |
| 1 January 1950 to 24 October 1951 | Refit: machinery overhaul, worn main and secondary guns replaced | [S10, pp. 204-205] |
| November 1951 | Post-refit trials fired nine rounds per gun from the main battery, the last time she fired the 380 mm guns | [S10, pp. 205-206] |
| May 1952 | Based at Toulon as flagship of CA Champion, training the fleet's gunners. A further refit ran October 1953 to February 1954 | [S10, pp. 205-206] |
| 30 January 1956 | Cruised with Jean Bart, the only time the two sisters were at sea together | [S10, pp. 205-206] |
| February 1956 | Laid up at Brest, then used as a stationary school and barracks ship | [S10, pp. 206-208] |
| 30 September 1967 | Struck from the naval register and renamed Q432 | [S10, pp. 206-208] [S12, p. 60] |
| 16 January 1968 | Condemned; sold in September 1968 to Cantieri Navali Santa Maria of Genoa. The four turret I guns were landed first. Towed to La Spezia and broken up over the following year | [S10, pp. 206-208] [S12, p. 60] |

## Jean Bart

| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 27 May 1936 | Contract awarded to Chantiers de Penhoët at Saint-Nazaire, with construction sub-contracted to the adjacent A. C. Loire yard | [S10, pp. 98-99, 152] |
| 12 December 1936 | Keel laid in A. C. Loire's new Caquot dock, known as the Jean Bart dock | [S10, pp. 98-99, 152] |
| 6 March 1940 | Launched | [S10, pp. 98-99, 152] |
| May to June 1940 | Fitting out abandoned in favour of getting her to sea. About 3,500 workers installed three boilers, two sets of turbines, two turbo generators and two pumps in a month. Outer shafts fitted 6 and 7 June; the three aft boilers were lit for the first time 12 to 14 June | [S10, pp. 152-154] |
| 11 June 1940 | CV Ronarc'h ordered to leave for Casablanca as soon as practical; he picked 19 June on the tides | [S10, pp. 152, 154] |
| 18 June 1940 | Two twin 90 mm Model 1930 mounts taken from the net layer Gladiateur were installed, without ammunition or directors | [S10, pp. 152-154] |
| 19 June 1940, 03:30 | Towed out by three tugs into a channel dredged to 8.5 m by 50 m instead of the intended 9 m by 70 m. She grounded and bent a screw blade; engines were started at 04:45. A He 111 hit her with a 100 kg bomb between the main battery turrets without significant damage. She met Le Hardi and Mameluk at 06:30 and left the area at 18:00 | [S10, pp. 152, 154] |
| 22 June 1940, 17:00 | Arrived at Casablanca | [S10, pp. 152, 154] [S16, p. 29] |
| 11 August 1940 | Moved into shallow water. CV Émile Barthes relieved Ronarc'h eleven days later | [S10, pp. 71-72, 154-155] |
| 7 September 1940 | Received sixteen 13.2 mm guns and seventeen 8 mm machine guns, of which only one machine gun was fitted | [S10, pp. 71-72, 154-155] |
| 1941 | An improvised director was built on a torpedo tube platform taken from the destroyer Enseigne Gabolde, carrying a 14 m rangefinder | [S10, pp. 71-72, 154-155] |
| 19 May 1942 | Turret I was fully operational and test fired. The improvised director worked poorly, so a triangulation system using observers at Dar Bouazza and Sidi Abderrahman was set up | [S10, pp. 155-156] |
| 6 October 1942 | The DEM radar was pronounced ready for service; tests had detected aircraft at up to 70 km | [S10, pp. 155-156] |
| 8 November 1942 | Operation Torch. Two bombs hit at 07:18; she fired four two-gun salvos between 07:08 and 07:19; 406 mm shells hit at 07:25, 07:37, twice at 08:06 and at 08:10. The jammed turret was cut free and back in action at 17:24 | [S10, p. 158] [S13, p. 81] |
| 8 November 1942 | The American account: Massachusetts received the "play ball" code at 07:04, fired nine full nine-gun salvos, 81 rounds, and hit five times within 16 minutes, jamming the turret drive train for eight hours | [S22] |
| 9 November 1942 | Her 90 mm guns fired on advancing ground troops | [S10, pp. 158-159] |
| 10 November 1942 | Engaged Augusta between 11:41 and 11:51 with two-gun salvos from the repaired turret. At 15:00 nine Ranger Dauntlesses with 1,000 lb bombs, covered by eight Wildcats, scored two hits; fires were out by 20:00 and she settled by the stern | [S10, pp. 158-159, 161] [S22] [S17, pp. 158-166] |
| 8 to 10 November 1942 | She fired 25 main battery shells in all and lost 22 killed and 22 wounded | [S10, pp. 159, 161] [S13, pp. 69-70] |
| 11 November 1942 | French forces in North Africa signed an armistice; repair work on the ship began the same day | [S10, pp. 150, 161] [S16, p. 210] |
| 15 February 1943 | Outboard turbines back in service and manual steering repaired | [S10, pp. 150, 161] [S13, p. 70] |
| 15 April to 6 May 1943 | Fenard reported her state to Horne on 15 April; Horne answered on 1 May that she could not be completed to her original design; on 6 May the US Navy agreed to take her over in September | [S10, pp. 150, 161] |
| September 1943 | Hull damage repaired. Sea trials from Casablanca with a deformed hull and a foul bottom reached 22.5 knots. She served as a training ship for the rest of the war | [S10, pp. 150, 161-162] [S13, pp. 33-34, 70, 112-115] [S17, pp. 253-257] |
| 18 August 1943 to March 1944 | Horne rejected both French completion proposals on 18 August; Fenard appealed to King on 8 December; King refused in March 1944 | [S10, pp. 150, 162, 181] |
| 22 February 1945 | The naval command decided she would be completed, without settling on battleship or carrier | [S10, p. 209] |
| 25 August 1945 | Left Casablanca for Cherbourg, arriving four days later | [S10, p. 209] |
| 21 September 1945 | The naval command ruled out discarding her, and then chose to finish her as a battleship on Richelieu's lines with a better anti-aircraft battery | [S10, pp. 209-210] |
| 11 March 1946 | Dry-docked at Brest for hull repair, machinery completion and turret installation. Hull work finished 26 November 1947 | [S10, p. 211] |
| 20 March to 9 October 1948 | Dry-docked again for bulges and propeller shaft repair. Machinery testing began 4 December 1948 | [S10, p. 211] |
| 1 May 1955 | Pronounced complete. That month she carried President Coty to Denmark and Oslo; in July she represented France in New York | [S10, pp. 213, 216-217] [S17, pp. 315-330] |
| 21 October 1955 | Replaced Richelieu as flagship of the training squadron at Toulon | [S10, p. 217] |
| 30 January 1956 | Cruised with Richelieu, the only time | [S10, pp. 205-206] |
| 26 October to 13 November 1956 | Operation Mousquetaire, with only the superfiring turret in operation. She left Toulon on 26 October, embarked troops at Algiers 29 to 31 October, reached Limassol on 4 November, fired four main battery rounds at Port Said on 5 November, and was back at Toulon on 13 November | [S13, pp. 74-75] [S10, pp. 212, 216-217, 221] [S17, pp. 337-342] |
| 11 to 19 July 1957 | Last period at sea and last firing of the main battery | [S10, pp. 221-222] |
| 1 August 1957 | Reduced to reserve, then used as a barracks ship from August 1957. Reserve status was downgraded on 1 January 1961 | [S10, pp. 221-222] [S13, pp. 76, 83] |
| 10 February 1970 | Struck from the naval register, renamed Q466 and offered for sale. Sold to shipbreakers on 21 May, towed to Brégaillon and dismantled | [S10, p. 222] [S13, pp. 76, 83] |

## Clemenceau

| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 17 January 1939 | Laid down in the Salou No. 4 dock at Brest, directly after Richelieu was launched from that dock the same day. Launch was slated for 1941 and completion for late 1943 | [S10, p. 171] |
| 28 September 1939 | Work halted by mobilisation, the available men going to Richelieu. Work resumed on 6 December | [S10, p. 171] |
| 10 June 1940 | Work halted again. About 10 percent of the hull, some 130 m of length, had been assembled. The yard workers flooded the drydock as German forces approached Brest | [S10, p. 171] |
| 1941 | Seized by the Germans and renamed Schlachtschiff R. Completion was considered and dropped for want of material and labour. The hull was floated out in 1941 to free the slipway | [S10, p. 171] [S13, p. 96] |
| 27 August 1944 | The hulk was sunk by Allied bombers | [S10, p. 171] |
| 23 February 1948 | The wreck was placed for sale. No buyer appeared, so the navy refloated her to clear the harbour bottom; she broke in half under tow and sank again. Salvors bought the wreck on 1 August 1951 | [S10, p. 171] |

## Conflicts between sources

- Date of the Swordfish torpedo attack on Richelieu at Dakar: 8 July 1940 [S10, p. 127] [S49] against the 6 July entry in the Hermes chronology [S33].
- Jean Bart's salvos at Augusta on 10 November 1942: nine two-gun salvos, three of them straddling [S10, pp. 158-159], against ten salvoes, repeatedly straddling [S22].
- Time Massachusetts opened fire on 8 November 1942: 07:40 [S21] against the "play ball" order at 07:04 [S22] and Jean Bart firing from 07:08 [S10, p. 158].
- Jean Bart's disposal: struck 10 February 1970 and sold 21 May 1970 [S10, p. 222] against "sold for scrap January 1970" [S35].
- Richelieu's guns after scrapping: two preserved, at Brest and at Ruelle [S10, pp. 206-208], against five preserved, at Brest, Gâvres, the École Navale, Ruelle and the La Spezia naval arsenal [S1].
- Clemenceau's hull after 1941: moored in front of the Brest U-boat pen [S10, p. 171] [S13, p. 96] against towed to Landévennec in the roadstead of Brest [S19, p. 78].
