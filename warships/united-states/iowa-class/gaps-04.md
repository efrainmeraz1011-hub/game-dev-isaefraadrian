# Gaps, facet 04 (sensors, fire control, and communications)

One entry per sub-facet with no source. Queries were run on 2026-09-12 against the registry in `skills/ww2-warship-research/SOURCES.md` and then the general web, following the facet 04 patterns in `QUERIES.md`.

Two registry sources failed for every query below and are not repeated in each entry:

- NAVSEA, BB-61 historical evaluation (2011), `BB61_DOE_FINAL.pdf`. Returned HTTP 403 to curl with a browser user agent and to WebFetch.
- HyperWar, Naval Ordnance and Gunnery NAVPERS 16116-A (March 1946). The index page loads and prints the full table of contents, but it carries no links to chapter pages and states that the volume is "under construction". The May 1944 edition on the Internet Archive was used instead.

Three questions that were open earlier in this session were closed by the Bureau of Ships type allowance RE-11A-100 (source S159) and are recorded here only so the search history is not lost: the sonar material allowed to a battleship, the electronic fit of the combat information center, and the number of secondary battery plotting rooms. What that document does not give is where any of those spaces were, and those parts stay below.

## Sonar room or sound room, and the compartment holding the sounding equipment

Queries run:
- `Iowa class battleship sonar`
- `USS New Jersey BB-62 sonar room battleship sonar equipment WWII`
- `battleshipnewjersey.org "sonar" battleship did battleships have sonar`
- `Iowa class battleship "no sonar" OR "sonar" underwater sound equipment fitted fast battleships USS Missouri QC` (restricted to navweaps.com, navalgazing.net, history.navy.mil, ibiblio.org, maritime.org, navsource.net, battleshipnewjersey.org, ussmissouri.org)
- `"USS Iowa" BB-61 sonar equipment installed 1943 1945 underwater sound`

Sources checked: NavSource photo indexes BB-61, BB-62, BB-63, BB-64 (no occurrence of "sonar" or "sound room" in any caption); navypedia Iowa class electronic equipment lines, as commissioned and for every listed modernization; HyperWar ship data page for BB-62; Battleship Missouri Memorial ship characteristics table; maritime.org document index, including its "Electricity, Communications, Sonar and Radar" section; Battleship New Jersey museum site search; BNJARS reference pages; NAVSEA BB-61 historical evaluation (403); archive.org item bb61gad1954, which proved to hold only a cover photograph and a damage control diagram rather than a full general arrangement set.

Result: **partly answered, and the remainder is a gap, 2026-09-12.** The type allowance settles what a battleship was allowed to carry under the heading "sonar material" on 1 August 1945: one Model NM series sounding equipment and one Model NK series portable sounding equipment, with no echo-ranging or listening set anywhere in the type BB sheets [S159, sheet 175F]. No fetched source names a sonar room, a sound room, or an underwater sound compartment in any ship of this class, gives a compartment number or deck for the NM or NK equipment, or reports a per-ship installation date. The dossier states the allowance and leaves the spaces as a gap.

## Deck and frame positions of the radio spaces

Queries run:
- `<ship> radio room transmitters antennas` per QUERIES.md, as `Battleship New Jersey museum "radio central" radio room BB-62 TBS TBL transmitters`

Sources checked: type allowance RE-11A-100 sheet 175A, which names radio central, the radio transmitter room, the emergency radio station, and the U.H.F. radio station "Radio IV or V" in a topside location, and lists their equipment but gives no compartment numbers; navy-radio.com BB-62 page and its visitor FAQ sheet, which gives the pre-1980s radio room as third deck just aft of the No. 2 barbette near sick bay but gives no room numbering and no split between transmitter and receiver rooms; nj2bb.org reference index, whose arrangement drawings and space list describe the preserved ship; NHHC photograph 80-G-469927, a dated December 1944 photograph of the New Jersey radio room whose caption names no equipment; HyperWar BB-62 ship data page, which lists no electronics.

Result: **partly answered, and the remainder is a gap, 2026-09-12.** The spaces and their equipment are sourced. Their compartment numbers and decks as built are not, and neither is any per-ship confirmation that a given hull held all of them.

## Deck and frame positions of the four plotting rooms

Queries run: `<ship> plotting room` per QUERIES.md.

Sources checked: Naval Ordnance and Gunnery 1944 chapter 17A3, which gives battleships either one plotting room with four computer and stable element groups or two rooms with two each and does not choose; type allowance RE-11A-100 sheets 175B and 175D, which list a main battery plotting room forward, a main battery plotting room aft, a secondary battery plotting room forward, and a secondary battery plotting room aft as four separate stations; NavSource BB-62, whose only plot caption is a 1983 photograph of "the forward secondary battery plot room"; NavSource BB-63, whose plot caption is a 1950 photograph of the main battery plotting room.

Result: **partly answered, and the remainder is a gap, 2026-09-12.** The count is sourced at four. No fetched source gives the deck or frame position of any of them.

## Mark 63 gun fire control system on this class

Queries run: covered by the facet 04 director searches and by the NavWeaps 40 mm page.

Sources checked: type allowance RE-11A-100 sheets 175D, 175E, 175F, where Mark 34-2 radar for the heavy machine gun battery appears under the BB 36, BB 38, BB 42, and BB 45-46 allowances but not under BB 60-62 or BB 63-64, and the heavy machine gun battery radar for these hulls is the Mark 29-2; navypedia class table and its full modernization list, which names Mark 3, Mark 4, Mark 8, Mark 12/22, Mark 13, Mark 25, Mark 27, Mark 35, and Mark 39 radars but no Mark 63; NavSource BB-61, BB-62, BB-63, BB-64, where the single mention of a Mark 63 with Mark 34 radar belongs to a Fletcher class destroyer photographed with New Jersey; NavWeaps 40 mm Bofors page, which calls the Mark 63 a post-war system.

Result: no source found, 2026-09-12, placing a Mark 63 director on a ship of this class. The dossier records the absence of evidence, not an absence of the equipment.
