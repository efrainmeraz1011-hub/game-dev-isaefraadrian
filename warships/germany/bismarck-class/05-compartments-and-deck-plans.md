# Bismarck class: compartments and deck plans

Status: partial (see gaps.md)

## The plans

The builder's plan booklet for Bismarck survives as Planheft Nr. 8, T.S. 509, Blohm & Voss Hamburg, a set of preliminary drawings (Vorläufige Zeichnung) at 1:200 stamped by the Oberkommando der Kriegsmarine, Amt für Kriegschiffbau, and marked secret under the law of 3 June 1914 [S31, title page]. Its contents list names the sheets: Längsschnitt (longitudinal section), Obere Ansicht (plan view), Aufbaudeck und Brücken (superstructure deck and bridges), Oberdeck, Batteriedeck, Zwischendeck, Panzerdeck, Oberes Plattformdeck, Mittleres Plattformdeck, Unteres Plattformdeck, Stauung (hold), Doppelboden (double bottom) and Querschnitte (cross sections) [S31, title page]. The longitudinal section carries the frame numbers along the keel and the section numbers I, II, III and onward from the stern, with the main turret elevation arcs drawn to 30 degrees [S31, Längsschnitt]. Compartment labels on the scanned sheets are too small to read at the resolution fetched; the section contents below come from the written sources. The Admiralty also worked from "an official sketch book, published by Blohm & Voss" taken from a prisoner [S15, §VII(21)].

The battery and armour decks were linoleum covered and held the crew spaces, the ship's company messing by division [S15, §VII(1)]. Officers' quarters were in the superstructure and deckhouse [S15, §VII(1)].

## Watertight subdivision and numbering

The hull was divided into 22 sections (Abteilungen) numbered I to XXII from stern to bow [S30, Abteilung; S3; S15, §VII(1)]. Seventeen sections, III to XIX, lay inside the citadel [S3]. The citadel's armoured transverse bulkheads stood at frame 32 aft and frame 202.7 forward [S3]. The torpedo bulkheads ran from Section III to Section XIX [S15, §VII(1)]; the double bottoms ran from Section VIII to Section XIV with hold compartments beyond [S15, §VII(1)]. Each section had its own ventilation system with two or more supply and two or more exhaust fans, some 200 AEG fans in all, with the ventilation workshop in Section XIII on the main deck [S15, §VII(13)]. Prisoners attributed the large complement to the elaborate subdivision [S15, §II]. The fuel cells, potable water and voids below the lower platform deck were "even more finely divided" than the platform decks [S3].

## Superstructure levels

The forward conning tower carried the forward command post with its 7 m rangefinder dome; the foretop command post sat above the foremast about 31 m above the sea; the after command post with its 10.5 m rangefinder stood abaft the mainmast; two SL-8 Flak directors in domes flanked the foremast and two open ones stood amidships aft; two 3 m night rangefinders stood either side of the Admiral's bridge [S6]. Prisoners placed the Flak directors A and B on the superstructure deck starboard and port forward, C in a tower on the deckhouse deck abaft the mainmast and D at its after end, the light Flak control at the foot of the mainmast (also a reserve signal station), and the forward light Flak control with the two quadruple 2 cm mounts in the gallery below the foretop director tower [S15, §VII(6)]. The AVKS placed the main Flak battle station in the foretop gallery with 23 to 25 men and no splinter protection, a forward night command post crowded with ship's command, searchlight and target-column crews, and an aft reserve Flak station with room for one commander and two order transmitters [S16, ¶69]. Searchlights: one on the foremast platform, four beside the funnel, two behind the mainmast [S8]. Hangars: a double hangar under the mainmast and single hangars either side of the funnel, with the catapult between mainmast and funnel [S7]; boats were stowed above the deckhouse hangar [S15, §VII(8)]. The 2001 wreck survey found the tower superstructure detached at the lower mast deck, inverted up-slope from the hull, with foretop, searchlight decks and Admiral's bridge still attached, and the funnel missing but its base, baffling and diesel exhaust pipes present [S28, wreck observations].

## Boats

KBismarck.com lists the boat outfit with weights, lengths and stowage frames: four 11.25 m picket boats of 8 mt (frames 103.1 and 101.0, S and P), three 11.52 m motor launches of 7 mt (frame 130.6), one 9.2 m motor yawl of 3.3 mt (frame 130.0, starboard), six 8.30 m class II cutters of 1.8 mt (frames 128.8, 142.7, 108.1), one 6.5 m class I yawl of 1.0 mt and two 6.5 m class II yawls of 1.6 mt, two 4 m dinghies of 0.2 mt, and 16 life rafts; the four cutters, yawls and two dinghies on the upper deck were landed before Rheinübung, leaving four picket boats, two motor launches, one motor yawl, two cutters and two yawls [S8, boats]. Two 12-ton cranes stood amidships and two smaller cranes under the funnel searchlight platform [S8, cranes].

## Conflicts between sources

- The deck between the battery deck and the platform decks: "Zwischendeck" on the plans [S31] and "Panzerdeck / main deck" in the interrogation report [S15, §VII(1)]; KBismarck.com calls the third armoured deck the Panzerdeck [S30]. The sources do not say whether Zwischendeck and Panzerdeck name the same deck.
- Armoured bulkhead positions: frames 32 and 202.7 [S3] against section boundaries II/III and XIX/XX [S15, §VII(2)].
