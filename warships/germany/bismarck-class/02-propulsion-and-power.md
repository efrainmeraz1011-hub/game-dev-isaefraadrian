# Bismarck class: propulsion and power

Status: partial (see gaps.md)

## Boilers

Twelve Wagner Hochdruck (high pressure) boilers stood in six watertight boiler rooms amidships, in sections XI and XIII [S5, boilers]. Each room held two boilers [S5]:

Prisoners described the boilers as destroyer-type high-pressure water-tube boilers on the "Le Mont" system with a high degree of superheat, working at about 800 lb/sq in (56 kg/cm²) and 450 degrees C; superheated steam was supplied to the turbines at 55 to 58 kg/sq cm (782 to 824 lb/sq in) (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(3)]. Each boiler had one oil fuel heater and two "Saacke" sprayers of a little over 1.5 tons/hour each, fitted diagonally; stokeholds could be run open at moderate speed or closed at high speed and generally ran open; each boiler room had one blower for direct air to the sprayers and two forced-draught fans, two oil fuel supply pumps (one steam, one electric) and three feed pumps (two steam, one electric) [S15, §VII(3)]. Auxiliary boilers at 180 lb/sq in (12.7 kg/cm²) in Section XIV supplied cooking and heating steam [S15, §VII(3)]. Between the main boiler rooms, Section XII held three boiler auxiliary machinery rooms (Kesselhilfsmaschinenräume) [S15, §VII(3)].

In action the starboard, port and centre boilers were isolated from each other and connected to their own turbine room, an arrangement called "Gefechtsschaltung" [S15, §VII(3)]. The war diary logs this as "Gefechtsgrundschaltung" (basic battle configuration), for example steam at all powerplants for 19 knots with boilers Nos. 1 and 3 on line per powerplant [S17, entries of September and October 1940].

## Turbines and gearing

Three Blohm & Voss turbine sets each drove one shaft; the centre turbine room was in section VIII and the port and starboard rooms in section X [S5, turbines]. Each set comprised an HP turbine (a Curtis wheel with 40 stages), a double-flow 15-stage IP reaction turbine, a 9-stage LP reaction turbine with the condenser slung below it, a single Curtis-wheel HP astern turbine and a divided double-flow LP astern turbine; no cruising turbine was fitted in Bismarck [S5]. HP and IP turbines ran at 2,800 rpm and the LP at 2,400 rpm at full power [S5]. KBismarck.com gives turbine and shaft speeds from the Brown Boveri sets in Tirpitz [S5, turbine performance table and footnote 2]:

| Turbine section | Turbine rpm | Shaft rpm |
|---|---|---|
| HP | 2,880 | 270 |
| IP | 2,880 | 270 |
| LP | 2,430 | 190 |
| HP astern | 2,025 | 180 |
| LP astern | 1,715 | 190 |
| Cruising turbine (Tirpitz only) | 4,220 | 170 |

Prisoners described Curtis-type turbines geared to the shaft at an average ratio stated as seven to one, with HP and IP on opposite sides of the shaft at the after end of the room and the LP forward in line with the IP (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(3)]. An Askania automatic regulating system worked by oil pressure on a telemotor system linked the steam regulating valves to the oil sprayer valves and feed pumps so that steam supply followed shaft revolutions; one operating and one reserve electric oil pump per turbine room served it [S15, §VII(3)]. The centre room had an electric circulating pump (3,000 rpm, 60 to 80 amps) and the wing rooms steam circulating pumps; each room had a rotary electric condensate pump (40 amps) discharging to hotwells in Section XII, an electric turbine-oil centrifuge and rotary bilge pumps (60 amps) [S15, §VII(3)]. Thrust blocks sat in Section IX (wing shafts) and Section VII (centre shaft) with electric lubricating pumps [S15, §VII(3)]. The centre shaft tunnel ran from Section II to Section VI and the wing tunnels from Section III to Section VII, each in four compartments [S15, §VII(3)].

## Shafts and propellers

Three three-bladed propellers of 4.70 m diameter, each 2.4 m³ in volume, turned at 270 rpm at full speed; port and centre screws turned counter-clockwise, starboard clockwise [S5, propellers]. Prisoners described them as phosphor bronze [S15, §VII(3)]. The three propellers were installed in floating dry dock V-VI between 23 June and 14 July 1940 [S20].

## Steering gear

Two parallel rudders of 24.21 m² each, inclined with 8 degrees divergence toward the centreline, were electrically controlled; each rudder with its hanging attachment had a volume of 11.63 m³ [S5, rudders]. The rudder gear was in Section I below the armour deck, starboard and port; the rudder motors and hand steering gear were in Section II on the upper platform deck; one motor could work both rudders but both ran in action, controlled electrically from the steering positions [S15, §VII(19)]. Seven steering positions existed: bridge (Friedenstand), Admiral's bridge, upper conning tower, lower conning tower (Kommandozentrale), after control tower, turret "B" (emergency action position) and the hand-steering compartment [S15, §VII(19)]. Two Anschütz gyro compasses stood in Section VIII (middle platform deck, starboard) and next to the lower conning tower in Section XIV (upper platform deck), fed at 55 to 65 volts AC, driving 15 to 20 repeaters [S15, §VII(19)].

The torpedo hit of 26 May 1941 jammed both rudders at 12 degrees to port [S20]; prisoners put the jam at between 10 and 15 degrees and said divers later centred one rudder while the other stayed jammed, after which the best speed on engines alone was 10 to 12 knots [S15, §V(3)]. The reconstructed war diary records the manual rudder being coupled in and the ship held head to sea by reversing screws [S17, 26 May, 2030 entry].

## Electrical plant

Total output was 7,910 kW at 220 volts from eight 500 kW diesel generators, five 690 kW turbo-generators and one 460 kW turbo-generator in four plants (E-Werke) [S5, electrical power plant]:

| Plant | Section | Generators | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starboard E-Werk 1 | VIII | 4 x 500 kW diesel | 2,000 kW |
| Port E-Werk 2 | VIII | 4 x 500 kW diesel | 2,000 kW |
| E-Werk 3 (starboard) and 4 (port) | XIV | 5 x 690 kW turbo, 1 x 460 kW turbo | 3,450 + 460 kW |
| Total | | | 7,910 kW |

A separate diesel motor room No. 1 (WE-E-Werk 1) in Section VII, starboard, held a Motorenwerke Mannheim type MWM RS 38 S six-cylinder diesel (serial 170093 in Bismarck, 170094 in Tirpitz) rated 460 ehp at 600 rpm with a 20% overload to 550 ehp for 30 minutes, driving a 550 kVA Garbe-Lahmeyer alternator type P23 FA925 10 Spez. B2 (serial 377071; 376848 in Tirpitz) [S5]. Prisoners gave the diesel generators as MWM six-cylinder, non-supercharged, 525 rpm, the turbo-generators as Garbe Lahmeyer at 8,000 rpm, each machine about 2,000 amperes for a total of 28,000 amps DC at 220 volts; the diesel switch rooms were in Section IX on the lower platform deck and the turbo-generator switch rooms (E-Schaltraum 3 and 4) in Section XV on the lower platform deck [S15, §VII(4)]. The electrical officer's action station (E-Gefechtsstand) was in Section XIV on the upper platform deck with a reserve station in switch room 2, Section IX, port [S15, §VII(4)].

Cable passages (Kabelgänge) about 2 m wide ran the length of the ship inside the torpedo bulkheads on the upper and lower platform decks from Section III to Section XVII, carrying emergency cables as well [S15, §VII(4)]. In action the generators were not paralleled on a ring main but fed units independently, with switching that let any generator group supply any turret or equipment [S15, §VII(4)]. Consumption figures given by a prisoner: cranes 400 to 500 amps each, fuel oil storage pumps 500 amps, fire pumps 80 amps, main turrets 2,000 amps each [S15, §VII(4)]. The AVKS measured the turret traversing-machinery starter drawing 623 amps in its first step and 1,180 amps in the second, and 925 amps maximum while firing one barrel during traverse [S16, ¶5]. AC at 220 volts 50 cycles came from the diesel alternator in Section VII and a second alternator driven off a turbo-generator in E-Werk 4, plus four rotary converters; AC served fire control, R/T and gyro compasses, with 380 volt three-phase for fire control [S15, §VII(4)]. Lighting used 40 watt Osram lamps and did not fail completely in the final action; portable accumulator lamps and dry-battery torches backed it up, and a battery charging room for emergency lighting stood in Section XII on the main deck [S15, §VII(4)]. The 14-inch hit of 24 May 1941 put E-Werk 4 out of action [S17, 24 May, 0620 and 0801 entries].

## Distilling plant

One evaporating plant (Frischwassererzeuger) with five electric pumps in the centre turbine room made boiler feed water; feed water tanks were in the double bottoms under the wing turbine rooms; a second distilling plant with electric pumps was in the auxiliary boiler room in Section XIV [S15, §VII(3)]. The standard displacement build-up carries 155 mt of drinking water, 510 mt of washing water and 752 mt of boiler feedwater at full load [S2].

Normal stowage bunkers were in the double bottoms and in the compartments outside the torpedo bulkheads up to the armour deck slope, with empty protective cells outboard; the "action" or ready-use bunkers were in the double bottoms under the boiler rooms in Sections XI and XIII; trimming cells in Sections I to IV and XX to XXII could take reserve fuel [S15, §VII(21)]. Fuel consumption at 18 knots was a little under two-thirds of a ton per boiler per hour and over three tons at full speed [S15, §VII(3)]. After the Denmark Strait hits, bunkers in Sections XIV and XV took water and fuel in Sections XX to XXII was cut off by flooded suction valves, so about 1,000 tons became unavailable [S15, §VII(21)]. On 26 May 1941 at 1903 the Fleet Commander signalled "Fuel situation urgent. When can I count on replenishment?", which Group West found inexplicable because it assumed sufficient fuel; the diary explains that the short-signal book's limited vocabulary shaped the wording [S17, 26 May, 1903 and 2007 entries].

## Underway replenishment

Fuelling at sea from tankers was exercised frequently in the Baltic [S15, §VII(21); S15, §IV]. The war diary timeline records an oil transfer exercise at sea with Prinz Eugen north of Hela on 13 May 1941 [S20]. Prisoners described 8,000-ton turbine tankers of 25 knots, fuelling at 15 knots with the tanker ahead, a 6 in hose in 5 m sections with a wire hawser through eyelets at each coupling taking the strain, 150 to 200 m of hose led from the tanker's stern through Bismarck's hawse pipe, hose pressure to 140 lb/sq in, and two electric geared oil fuel storage pumps (Section VI lower platform deck starboard, Section XV lower platform deck port) of 6,000 rpm and 460 amps at 150 lb/sq in that, working with the tanker's pumps, could empty an 8,000-ton tanker in 12 hours; fuel stand pipes passed through Section XXI and the after part of the ship [S15, §VII(21)]. On 10 March 1942 Tirpitz transferred 1,422 tons of fuel oil to the destroyers Z25 and Hermann Schoemann and torpedo boats T5 and T12 by 0500, and another 300 tons to Friedrich Ihn between 1300 and 1600 [S19].

## Compressed air, anchors and capstans

Two diesel-driven air compressors on the main deck, one starboard and one port with their air reservoirs, fed a ring main used for portable salvage pumps, catapults, sirens and tools [S15, §VII(3)]. Electric capstan motors stood in the "Bugspillraum" in Section XX with the cable lockers in Section XXI [S15, §VII(3)]. KBismarck.com gives four anchors (three at the bow, one at the stern to port), each 9,500 kg including the shackle ring, with 72 mm chain of which a 25 m length weighed 2,806 kg and a permitted load strain of 99,751 kg [S8]; prisoners said the ship carried two 6-ton stockless anchors [S15, §VII(3)].

## Conflicts between sources

- Maximum power and speed: 150,170 hp and 30.12 knots [S1; S5] against prisoners' 135,000 shp and 32.5 knots on trials [S15, §VII(3)].
- Fuel capacity: 7,900 mt [S1], 8,294 mt [S5], 8,500 tons [S15 Admiralty estimate], 8,000 tons [S15 von Müllenheim-Rechberg], 12,000 tons [S15 fuel pump rating]. Weights page total oil at maximum displacement is 6,194 + 527 + 1,726 mt [S2].
- Turbine speeds: 2,800 rpm HP and IP, 2,400 rpm LP [S5, text] against 2,880 and 2,430 rpm in the Tirpitz Brown Boveri table [S5, table].
- Gear ratio: prisoners' "seven to one" [S15] against 2,880 turbine rpm to 270 shaft rpm in the table [S5], which is about 10.7 to 1.
- Anchors: four of 9,500 kg [S8] against two 6-ton stockless anchors [S15].
- Diesel generator speed: 600 rpm for the MWM RS 38 S alternator engine [S5] against 525 rpm stated for the diesel generators by prisoners [S15].
