# Yamato class: service history

Status: covered

Timeline only. Design, damage mechanics and equipment changes belong to facets 01 to 07. Dates and times are as the cited source prints them; combinedfleet.com gives Japanese local time unless it marks a zone.

Constructor Rear Admiral Fukuda Keiji's design team completed the final design in March 1937 [S1, S2]. The US Naval Technical Mission was told the design of the gun began when the 46 cm calibre was selected in 1934 and that design of the mount was completed and production commenced in 1939 to 1940 [S11, p. 9]. The mission was also told that three 18-inch battleships were originally projected: Yamato, Musashi and Shinano [S11, p. 9].

Before the sortie Yamato embarked 1,170 rounds for the 18.1-inch guns, 1,620 rounds for the secondary guns, 13,500 anti-aircraft rounds and 11.5 million rounds for smaller calibres [S1]. The NHHC essay gives the same load with 1,629 rounds for the 6.1-inch guns [S24]. Sixty-seven naval cadets of Etajima class No. 74 were sent ashore on 5 April, and the sick and some older sailors on 6 April [S1].

Musashi sank in 4,430 feet of water at 13-07N, 122-32E as given by her own damage action report; the DesDiv 17 report gives 12-50N, 122-35E and Kiyoshimo's log 12-48N, 122-41.5E [S2]. Kiyoshimo and Hamakaze rescued 1,423 survivors; 1,023 of her 2,399-man crew were lost, including her commanding officer Rear Admiral Inoguchi [S2].

Paul Allen's expedition team found Musashi in March 2015 under the Sibuyan Sea [S25].

## Shinano, 1940 to 1944

Shinano was laid down as the third Yamato-class hull and completed as an aircraft carrier. The US Naval Technical Mission recorded her as "built on a Yamato Class hull" [S12, p. 25].

The Technical Mission gives her complement as about 1,900, of whom roughly 75 per cent had sea experience, and notes that air tests and the sealing of holes cut for cables, pipes and ventilation were incomplete when she sailed [S12, pp. 25 to 26].

## Conflicts between sources

- Time of Yamato's magazine explosion. combinedfleet gives 1423 [S1]; the NHHC essay gives capsizing complete by 1420 and the No. 1 magazine detonating at 1430 [S24]; S-06-2 gives the capsize at about 1420 and the explosion at about 120 degrees of roll without a clock time [S12, p. 14].
- Yamato's survivors. combinedfleet gives 276 rescued and 3,055 of 3,332 lost [S1]; the NHHC essay gives 3,055 of 3,332 lost and cites Morison for 23 officers and 246 enlisted surviving [S24]; S-06-2 says about 280 survived, including 23 officers and warrant officers, all from topside [S12, p. 14].
- Musashi's torpedo and bomb hits. Her chief of staff reported 18 torpedoes and 40 bombs; the action report lists 21 torpedo hits; the Technical Mission identified ten certain and four possible torpedo hits [S12, pp. 1, 17]. The National Museum of the US Navy page says approximately 19 torpedoes and 17 bombs [S25].
- Musashi's complement. combinedfleet gives a 2,399-man crew [S2]; the National Museum of the US Navy page says 2,500 sailors [S25].
- Yamato's secondary ammunition load for Ten-ichi-go. combinedfleet gives 1,620 rounds [S1]; the NHHC essay gives 1,629 [S24].
