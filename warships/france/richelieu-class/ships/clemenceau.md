# Clemenceau

Status: covered

The third ship. She was laid down, never launched, and never armed. The dated record is in 08-service-history.md.

## What differed from the class baseline

Darlan chose the Project A variant for this ship on 19 March 1938, which kept Richelieu's general layout. He took the less radical of the two designs because the redesign of the hull interior required by the Project B variant would have delayed construction by at least twelve months [S10, pp. 166-167].

### Secondary and anti-aircraft batteries rearranged

The number of triple 152 mm turrets was cut to four, two as amidships wing turrets and two on the centreline aft of the superstructure. The broadside was unchanged, since three turrets could still bear on either side [S10, pp. 166-167]. The weight saved allowed twelve 100 mm Model 1937 guns in six fully enclosed twin turrets, against the open mounts of Richelieu, plus six twin 37 mm ACAD mounts [S10, pp. 166-170]. Internal compartments were kept as close to Richelieu as possible; the existing 152 mm magazines were modified to hold 100 mm and 37 mm ammunition [S10, pp. 167, 171].

### Armour and displacement

The belt was reduced to 320 mm to hold displacement down [S10, pp. 166-167]. Standard displacement stayed at 35,000 long tons for the treaty, and full load rose to 44,800 t (44,100 long tons) because of the added anti-aircraft guns (as planned, 1938) [S10, pp. 167, 171].

### Aviation and crew

The hangar had to be shortened for the centreline 152 mm turrets, and was widened instead so that two aircraft could sit side by side, keeping Richelieu's aircraft complement [S10, pp. 166-167]. Complement rose to 1,670 officers and men (as planned, 1938) [S10, pp. 167, 171].

In every other respect, dimensions, propulsion, main battery and armour layout, she was to be the same ship [S10, pp. 167, 171].
