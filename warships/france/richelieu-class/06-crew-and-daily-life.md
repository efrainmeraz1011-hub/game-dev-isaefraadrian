The 1943 increase came mostly from the new anti-aircraft guns and radars [S10, p. 189]. The class article notes that complement "changed considerably" with the anti-aircraft battery [S10, pp. 99-101, 120-121].

## Commanders

Capitaine de vaisseau Marzin came aboard Richelieu on 15 October 1939 [S10, pp. 123-124]; CV Deramond relieved him on 27 February 1941 [S10, pp. 147-149]; CV Lambert took over on 29 April 1943 [S10, pp. 125, 181-182, 186]; CV Merveilleux du Vignaux on 31 May 1944 [S10, pp. 193-194, 199]. Jean Bart's first commander was CV Pierre-Jean Ronarc'h, relieved by CV Émile Barthes on 22 August 1940 [S10, pp. 71-72, 154-155].

## Crew composition and 1945

On the way to Durban in June 1945 Richelieu stopped at Diego Suarez to land her non-white crewmen at the request of the South African government, which the crew resented [S10, pp. 199-200]. In Indochina from October 1945 she served as staging area, hospital, artillery support and troop transport, and landed a party to fight ashore [S10, pp. 200-201].

## Boats

Eight motor boats, two motor launches, three motor pinnaces, two whale boats, two dinghies and two small flat-bottomed boats [S10, pp. 99-101, 120-121].
