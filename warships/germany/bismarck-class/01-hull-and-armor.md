# Bismarck class: hull and armor

Status: partial (see gaps.md)

Bismarck is the documented ship. Tirpitz figures are given where a source states them. KBismarck.com does not date its tables; those numbers are marked (source undated). The 1941 interrogation report records what prisoners said and what the Admiralty estimated from a Blohm & Voss sketch book; those are marked (prisoner statements, 1941).

Hull coefficients as computed by KBismarck.com from the figures above: length/beam 6.71, beam/draught 3.85, block coefficient 0.55 (44,900 mt underwater volume over 241.55 x 36.00 x 9.33), midship coefficient 0.97 (326 m² midship section), waterplane coefficient 0.66, prismatic coefficient 0.57 [S1]. The propulsion page gives the prismatic coefficient as 0.56 [S5].

Tirpitz (source undated): waterline length 241.55 m, overall length 250.5 m, beam 36 m, maximum draft 10.61 m, depth 15 m [S19]. The US Naval Technical Mission gave Tirpitz as she lay on 12 November 1944: length between perpendiculars 790 feet (240.8 m), length overall 821.5 feet (250.4 m), moulded beam 118.0 feet (36.0 m), drafts 29.0 feet (8.8 m) forward and aft [S18, principal characteristics].

Prisoners gave Bismarck's length as 243 metres (797 ft) and beam as 36 metres, possibly 38; the draught when she sailed from Gotenhafen short of fuel was put at not less than 11 metres and possibly 11.60 metres fully loaded (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(1)].

The hull was almost entirely welded with practically no rivets (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(1)]. Bilge keels were fitted; there were no bulges, anti-rolling tanks or stabilisers [S15, §VII(1)]. The ship rolled somewhat in heavy seas and pitched little [S15, §VII(1)]. Vibration peaked at 21 knots and was most noticeable aft [S15, §VII(3)]. The plans booklet's longitudinal section shows the frame numbers running along the keel and the watertight sections numbered with Roman numerals from I at the stern [S31, Längsschnitt].

The build-up from empty ship to maximum is in the weights section below [S2]. The KBismarck.com comparison table gives full load as 51,700 mt for 1941 [S1]. Tirpitz: standard 43,900 mt, full load maximum 53,500 mt (source undated) [S19]. On 12 November 1944 Tirpitz displaced about 42,000 tons with roughly 3,000 tons of fuel and fresh water aboard and minor flooding at bow and stern [S18, §3(e)].

Prisoners mostly insisted on the declared 35,000 tons; one who had been aboard during building said the German authorities counted that figure without fuel, feed water, domestic water and stores and with only half the ammunition, from which the Admiralty concluded that deep displacement was not far short of 50,000 tons (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(1)].

## Vertical protection

The main belt was 320 mm KC, 4.8 m wide and 170.7 m long, running from frame 32 to frame 202.7 and covering 70% of the waterline [S3, vertical protection]. It was backed by 50 mm of teak and bolted to 16 to 25 mm side plating [S3]. As designed 3.3 m of the belt stood above the waterline and 1.5 m below; in practice the split was 2.6 m above and 2.2 m below [S3]. Above the belt the citadel side was 145 mm KC up to the armoured upper deck, reduced to 120 mm between frames 186.7 and 202.7 [S3]. Forward of the belt the side was 60 mm Wh and aft of it 80 mm Wh [S3]. The belt followed the hull curvature and was inclined outboard 17 degrees abreast turret Anton, 10 degrees abreast Bruno, 7 degrees abreast Dora and 8 to 10 degrees abreast Cäsar; the cambered sections were about 40% of belt length [S3].

Transverse armoured bulkheads closed the citadel at frame 202.7 forward (145 mm at battery and armour deck level, 220 mm at the upper platform deck, 180 mm at the middle platform deck) and at frame 32 aft, of similar thickness [S3]. The main belt weighed 5,149 mt, the bulkhead armour at frames 10.5, 32 and 202.7 weighed 372 mt [S2].

Prisoners put the belt at 20 cm (7.9 in) at its thickest, about 5 metres broad, from the bulkhead between Sections XIX and XX to that between Sections II and III, with thinner tapering ("Abstufung") above and below; they put the transverse bulkheads at 20 cm from upper deck to lower platform deck (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(2)]. The Admiralty flagged these estimates as to be treated with reserve [S15, §VII(2)].

## Horizontal protection

Deck thicknesses by region [S3, horizontal protection table]:

| Deck | Over machinery | Over magazines | Bow to frame 233 | Stern to frame 10.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upper deck (Wh) | 50 mm | 50 to 80 mm | 25 to 50 mm | 50 mm |
| Battery deck (St 52) | 6 to 20 mm | 6 to 20 mm | 6 to 12 mm | 6 to 12 mm |
| Armour deck, centre to slopes (Wh) | 80 to 110 mm | 100 to 120 mm | none | 110 mm |
| Upper platform deck (Wh) | none | none | 20 mm | none |
| Total, centre to slopes (Wh) | 130 to 160 mm | 150 to 200 mm | 45 to 70 mm | 160 mm |

The upper deck ran from frame 10.5 to frame 224 and was planked with 68 mm of teak; the 80 mm plating lay around the secondary turrets [S3]. The battery deck sat 2.4 m below the upper deck [S3]. The armour deck was 10.3 m above the keel, flat amidships 1 m above the designed waterline and sloped outboard at about 22 degrees to meet the lower edge of the belt below the waterline; the slopes were 110 mm around the machinery and 120 mm beside the magazines [S3]. The stern turtle deck of 110 mm covered the steering gear and the bow between frames 202.7 and 233 had a 20 mm upper platform deck [S3]. Prisoners called the armour deck 20 cm and the upper deck 12 cm (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(2)]. Funnel uptakes were armoured to the upper deck with armoured gratings at the armour deck (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(2)].

## Turrets, barbettes and command posts

Main turrets were 130 to 360 mm KC; barbettes 340 mm KC above the upper deck and 220 mm below it down to the armour deck [S3, turret protection]. NavWeaps, citing Brower, breaks the turret down: lower face 36 cm, upper face 18 cm, lower sides 22 cm, upper sides 15 cm, lower rear 32 cm, upper rear 18 cm, roof 13 cm, all KC [S9, mount/turret data]. Secondary turrets were 20 to 100 mm Wh with 80 mm barbettes above the upper deck and 20 mm below [S3]. Barbette armour for four 38 cm and six 15 cm turrets weighed 2,306 mt and the turret armour 1,598 mt [S2].

Command posts [S3, command posts table]:

| Post | Walls | Roof | Floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forward conning tower | 350 mm KC | 220 mm KC | 70 mm |
| After conning tower | 150 mm KC | 50 mm KC | 30 mm |
| Foretop | 60 mm KC | 20 mm KC | 20 mm |

The forward rangefinder cupola had 200 mm walls and a 100 mm roof; its communications shaft to the armour deck was 85 cm in diameter with 220 mm walls; the after shaft was 70 cm in diameter with 50 mm walls; the after cupola had 100 mm walls and a 50 mm roof; the foretop cupola 30 mm walls and 20 mm roof [S3]. Conning tower armour weighed 492 mt [S2].

## Torpedo defense system

Port and starboard longitudinal torpedo bulkheads of 45 mm Ww ran vertically (not sloped as in Scharnhorst) [S3, underwater protection]. Their distance from the shell [S3, table]:

| Station | Bulkhead to outer hull | Bulkhead |
|---|---|---|
| Turret A, frame 192.55 | 3 m | 45 mm Ww |
| Turret B, frame 174.35 | 3.5 m | 45 mm Ww |
| Midship, frame 120.8 | 5.4 m | 45 mm Ww |
| Turret C, frame 64.35 | 3.8 m | 45 mm Ww |
| Turret D, frame 46.15 | 3 m | 45 mm Ww |

Outboard of the bulkhead sat an empty gas-expansion and counterflooding space, then three liquid-loaded compartments of fuel oil and feed water abutting the bulkhead [S3]. The system was designed against 250 kg (550 lb) of TNT [S3]; the US Naval Technical Mission reported Tirpitz's system as designed to withstand about 660 pounds (300 kg) of German hexanite [S18, §3(b); S3, footnote 2]. Prisoners described the bulkheads as about 5.5 m inside the belt from Section III to Section XIX, from battery deck to bottom, 5 cm thick, with an outer empty layer (Flutzelle) below the armour deck slope and an inner layer used for fuel (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(1), (2)]. The Tirpitz report noted that the empty counterflooding tanks lay outboard of the wing liquid tanks, so rupturing them produced a larger listing moment than an inboard void would have [S18, §3(e)].

The double bottom was 1.7 m deep between frames 77.3 and 154.6 [S3]; prisoners placed the double bottoms from Section VIII to Section XIV inclusive, extending to the slope of the armour deck, with hold compartments beyond [S15, §VII(1)]. An MES (Magnetischer Eigenschutz) degaussing cable system was fitted [S3]; the ship was degaussed by coils (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(17)].

## Subdivision

The hull was divided into 22 watertight sections, 17 of them (III to XIX) inside the citadel [S3, underwater protection]. Between armour deck and upper deck, 30 mm Wh splinter bulkheads 3 to 5.4 m inboard of the belt formed 51 cells, doubled to 102 by the battery deck; the upper platform deck had over 250 compartments, the middle platform deck nearly as many and the lower platform deck over 200 [S3]. Section-by-section contents are in 05-compartments-and-deck-plans.md.

## What the wreck shows

The survey cited by KBismarck.com found only two complete penetrations of the lower 320 mm belt, both on the starboard side, credited to Rodney's 40.6 cm guns because King George V stayed on the port side [S22, footnote 3]. The July 2001 Channel 4 survey saw many shell impacts, many penetrations of lightly armoured areas, few penetrations of the armoured belts, and several large openings beneath the belts on both sides identified as British torpedo hits widened during the slide down the seamount; the stern was shorn off and the starboard rudder was the only one remaining [S28, wreck observations]. Prisoners, all but one junior officer, stated that no shells penetrated the belt; the majority denied that the armour deck was pierced, one officer alleging penetration near the W/T room [S15, §V(3), §VI].

## Conflicts between sources

- Waterline length and beam: 241.55 m and 36.00 m [S1] against prisoners' 243 m and 36 or 38 m [S15, §VII(1)].
- Fuel capacity feeding the displacement build-up: 7,900 mt in the general data table [S1], 8,294 mt on the propulsion page [S5], and 6,194 + 527 + 1,726 mt in the weights build-up [S2]; the Admiralty estimated 8,500 tons including 1,750 tons of reserve bunkers [S15, §VII(21)]. See 02-propulsion-and-power.md.
- Prismatic coefficient: 0.57 [S1] against 0.56 [S5].
- Belt thickness: 320 mm [S3] against prisoners' 20 cm [S15, §VII(2)]; armour deck 80 to 120 mm [S3] against prisoners' 20 cm [S15, §VII(2)]; upper deck 50 to 80 mm [S3] against prisoners' 12 cm [S15, §VII(2)].
- Forward armoured bulkhead position: frame 202.7 [S3] against "junction of Sections XIX and XX" [S15, §VII(2)]; aft bulkhead frame 32 [S3] against "junction of Sections II and III" [S15, §VII(2)]. The two descriptions may coincide if those frames are the section boundaries; neither source says so.
- Torpedo system design charge: 250 kg TNT for Bismarck [S3] against 300 kg hexanite for Tirpitz [S18, §3(b)].
- Full load displacement: 51,760 mt [S1, general layout] against 51,700 mt [S1, comparison table].
