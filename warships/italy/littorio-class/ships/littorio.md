# Littorio, renamed Italia on 30 July 1943

Lead ship of the class, built by Ansaldo at Genoa-Sestri Ponente, laid down 28 October 1934, launched 22 August 1937, completed 6 May 1940 [S25, pp. 34, 46].

## Name and renaming

The name is that of the Lictor, the bearer of the Roman fasces adopted as the emblem of Italian Fascism [S28, p. 171]. The ship took the name Italia on 30 July 1943, after the fall of Mussolini's government [S7; S20]. A different date for the renaming, 25 July 1943, is given in the English literature [S26, p. 403].

## Ways in which she differed from her sisters

Her 381 mm guns were assembled from six major components rather than the four of the other ships, and Ansaldo built them; two of the three turrets on Roma and all of Littorio's mountings were made by OTO [S1].

Her 152 mm battery was the Ansaldo Model 1934 in four triple mountings, where Vittorio Veneto and Roma had the OTO Model 1936 [S22; S2].

Hull length overall 237.76 m, the shorter of the two hull forms [S29, pp. 289-290].

She was the first of the class to carry radar: an EC 3 bis set in August 1941, an updated version in April 1942 that proved unsuccessful, and the EC 3 ter in September 1942 [S28, p. 172].

## Building and trials

Changes to the design and a shortage of armour plate delayed the launch by three months from the planned May 1937 [S25, pp. 117-119]. During fitting out the bow was modified to reduce vibration and wetness forward. Sea trials ran from 23 October to 21 December 1939. She commissioned on 6 May 1940 and joined the 9th Division at Taranto with Vittorio Veneto, under Rear Admiral Carlo Bergamini [S28, p. 172; S25, pp. 121-125].

The Italian official account records that Italia took one of the radio-controlled bombs in the same attack that sank Roma and shipped several hundred tonnes of sea water [S13, note 19].

## Service record in figures

49 missions and 12,452 miles between 10 June 1940 and 8 September 1943, then 3 missions and 2,427 miles to 25 April 1945 [S14, table 1]. The Marina Militare's summary gives 46 war missions, of which 9 were searches for the enemy and 3 protection of national traffic [S7].

## Fate

Interned in the Great Bitter Lake in Egypt after the armistice [S7; S13]. She left the Great Bitter Lake on 5 February 1947, was allocated to the United States under the peace treaty, and was stricken on 1 June 1948 and broken up at La Spezia [S28, pp. 175-178; S7].
