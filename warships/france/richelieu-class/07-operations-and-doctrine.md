## Dakar, July 1940: the torpedo hit and the damage control effort

Richelieu reached Dakar at 17:44 on 23 June 1940 with half her fuel gone [S10, pp. 124-126]. On 8 July Swordfish from HMS Hermes, escorted by HMS Dorsetshire and HMAS Australia, attacked her with torpedoes [S33]; the official French memorial chronology records "8 juillet 1940 : torpillage du Richelieu par les Anglais en rade de Dakar" [S41]. The night before, a motorboat from HMS Milford dropped four depth charges under her stern to wreck the screws, without effect; at 04:15 the Swordfish launched as Richelieu prepared to sortie with the eight rounds loaded in her guns to attack Hermes. One torpedo struck aft on the starboard side, tearing a 9.3 by 8.5 m hole between the shafts; two directors were thrown off their tracks, the starboard shafts bent, and flooding followed. Damage control pumped fuel out of the bunkers to offset the lost buoyancy aft; the ship was towed in, ringed with anti-torpedo nets, and with some 2,400 t of water aboard sat on the bottom at low tide [S10, p. 127; S15, pp. 93-94; S16, p. 32]. The NHHC photograph of 25 July 1940 shows the buoys of those nets around her [S27].

Tankers came alongside to pump oil and lighten her, but water kept entering through the cable tunnels; pump hoses tore loose as she rose and fell. Dakar had no dry dock for her, so damaged bulkheads were patched and pumped one by one; 1,300 t of water remained on 28 August and the pumps kept breaking down. Marzin's report blamed insufficient pumping equipment, poor welding of bulkheads and turret trunks that were not watertight [S10, pp. 128, 137]. Directors were re-seated, wiring replaced, generators rebuilt; Admiral de Laborde flew in to inspect and organise the defence, and Plançon and Cayla were replaced [S10, pp. 137-138]. An 11.5 m square patch was fabricated from scavenged metal for 10 September, and a steel cofferdam was begun; 150 complete 380 mm charges were remade from Strasbourg's 330 mm stock; the 100 mm, 37 mm and 13.2 mm guns were kept manned continuously against air attack [S10, pp. 138-139]. The patch failed to seal on 10 October; the cofferdam, built with a void that served as a ballast tank so it could be floated into place and sunk, was ready by late December; the hull was pumped dry, sealed with welded plates and cement, and closed by 28 February 1941 [S10, pp. 147-149].

## Dakar, September 1940: gunnery under fire and the turret II explosions

Operation Menace put Barham, Resolution, Ark Royal, four cruisers and Free French transports off Dakar on 23 September 1940 [S10, pp. 139-141; S32]. Richelieu's 100 mm guns fired warning shots at the Free French aviso Savorgnan de Brazza after 07:00 and at the sloops Commandant Dominé and Commandant Duboc at 08:10 [S10, pp. 139-141; S16, p. 42]. Barham and Resolution opened on her at 11:05 in poor visibility and checked fire after twenty minutes; she was moored facing north and could not reply, so after they withdrew Marzin turned her with tugs until the main battery bore [S10, p. 141; S15, pp. 109-114]. Barham's chronology puts the bombardment between 11:00 and 11:30 on the 23rd [S31].

On 24 September three Swordfish and Skua strikes between 06:25 and 08:00 missed; her gunners claimed three of six aircraft shot down [S10, pp. 141-142; S15, pp. 137-139]. She returned fire at 09:40 with turret II, the crew of turret I being ashore at Cap Manuel [S1]. Gun No. 7 was shattered and No. 8 bulged, its rifling gashed for 8 m [S1]; the cause was first put on the remade Strasbourg propellant and in 1941 traced to the shell base design [S12, pp. 77-78; S10, pp. 143, 149]. Guns 5 and 6 stayed in action without a hit; at 09:57 a secondary gun hit Barham; the British broke off at 10:07 after some 160 shells had landed near the ship. Smoke was laid; from 12:56 guns 5 and 6 straddled a cruiser; four 380 mm rounds went at Barham at 13:11 to 13:12 without a hit [S10, pp. 143-145; S15, p. 140]. Overnight Marzin moved the crew of turret II to turret I, which also meant moving shells and charges between magazines [S10, pp. 143-145; S15, p. 140]. Barham's chronology records a bombardment on the 24th at 14,000 yards, Barham hit by two shells, and the assessment that 400 rounds of 15-inch had done little or no damage [S31]; Resolution's records that mist forced the bombarding ships in from a planned 26,000 yards, the maximum of the 15-inch guns at 20 degrees [S32].

On 25 September Richelieu opened at 09:04 with turret I, two shots falling short; the SD19 charges used that day gave less pressure and no prematures but also less range than the tables assumed, which upset the fire control [S1; S10, p. 147; S15, pp. 152-154]. Bévéziers torpedoed Resolution at 09:10; Barham straddled Richelieu and hit her amidships at 09:15 above the belt, without casualties; Richelieu hit Barham in the bow; the British disengaged at 09:25 [S10, p. 147; S15, pp. 152-154; S31]. In two days she fired 24 rounds of 380 mm without a hit [S1]. Clearing guns 5 and 6 afterwards by firing, No. 5 failed as No. 8 had and stuck in recoil at 15 degrees; No. 6 was cleared by hosing down the charges and removing charge and shell through the breech [S1]. Casualties in the harbour: 100 dead and 182 wounded in the ships, 84 dead and 197 wounded ashore [S10, p. 147; S16, p. 42]. Barham's hit did little structural harm but deformed interior bulkheads, pressed the armour deck down and cut the boiler uptakes and wiring [S10, pp. 147-149]. On 10 April 1942 gun No. 6 fired six rounds with the corrected shells without incident [S10, p. 150].

## The 1943 refit at New York Navy Yard

Richelieu ran her engines on trials from 25 to 29 January 1943, landed her aircraft gear and French anti-aircraft guns, and sailed from Dakar on 30 January with Montcalm at 14 knots and seven degrees of rudder, arriving at New York on 11 February; she entered Dock No. 5 at the Brooklyn Navy Yard on the 18th [S10, p. 150; S13, pp. 37, 50]. NHHC's caption dates her arrival in New York Harbor to 11 February 1943 [S45]; the Library of Congress caption calls her "the mighty 35,000-ton Richelieu" on her way up the East River to a navy yard "to be repaired and refitted" [S28]. Three shifts totalling about 2,000 men worked around the clock for five months [S10, pp. 125, 181-182, 186]. The turret II roof came off so three guns could be replaced by barrels from Jean Bart's turret I, brought from Casablanca; the cradles were sound [S10, pp. 186-187; S1]. Drawings of the 380 mm shell were made at Dakar and sent to the United States, where Crucible Steel took a contract for 930 shells [S10, pp. 186-187]. Firing trials in Chesapeake Bay ran from late August into mid September; machinery trials on 25 and 26 September; she was ready to sail on 14 October [S10, pp. 186, 188-189]. The Commons photographs of 26 August and 7 September 1943 show her at sea and at anchor in this period [S44]. Navypedia dates the work to 30 January to August 1943 and her joining the Home Fleet to 20 November 1943 [S35]; uboat.net gives February to October 1943 [S38].

## Home Fleet, November 1943 to March 1944

Fraser inspected her at Scapa Flow on 24 November 1943; a Type 284 gunnery radar was fitted while she trained with British units [S10, p. 190]. Operation Posthorn, 10 to 12 February 1944 with Anson and Furious off Norway, produced no surface action; a repeat was cancelled after two destroyers collided [S10, pp. 190-191; S16, pp. 307, 313]. In March the Allies judged five battleships against Tirpitz excessive; because she held only armour-piercing shells she went to the Eastern Fleet rather than to Normandy [S10, pp. 190-191].

## Eastern Fleet, 1944

She reached Trincomalee on 10 April 1944 [S10, p. 192; S29; S30]. Operation Cockpit sailed on 16 April: Force 69 was Queen Elizabeth (flag), Valiant and Richelieu with cruisers Newcastle, Nigeria, Ceylon, Gambia and Tromp and nine destroyers; the carriers Illustrious and Saratoga struck Sabang on 19 April [S29; S30]. Illustrious's chronology lists Richelieu with Queen Elizabeth, Valiant and Renown as TF 70 [S34], and an Australian account puts her in Force 70 with Renown and the carriers [S40]; Jordan and Dumas put her in Force 69 [S10, p. 192]. Japanese bombers counter-attacked and she engaged them with the 100 mm and 40 mm batteries [S10, p. 192; S16, p. 319]. Operation Transom (7 to 27 May, Surabaya) had TF 65 of Queen Elizabeth, Valiant and Richelieu with three cruisers and eight destroyers [S29; S30]; the fleet refuelled on 15 May [S10, pp. 192-193]. Operation Pedal (19 to 23 June, Port Blair) took only the fast ships [S10, p. 194; S16, p. 323].

Operation Crimson was the first bombardment. TF 62 sailed at 16:00 on 22 July with Queen Elizabeth, Valiant, Richelieu, Renown, Illustrious, Victorious, Cumberland, five light cruisers and destroyers; at 03:00 on the 25th the bombardment group of the four capital ships, Cumberland, Ceylon, Kenya, Nigeria, Gambia and Tromp detached [S29; S30]. Shooting practice on 7, 14 to 15 and 17 July preceded it [S10, p. 194]. Richelieu was last in line astern of Renown. Queen Elizabeth opened at 06:54; Corsairs spotted; Richelieu fired four-gun salvos, two guns per turret, hit with her second salvo, wrecked buildings and the power station, and her secondary turrets silenced a coastal battery firing on Tromp. Firing stopped at 07:15; she had fired 81 APC, a salvo every 50 seconds, nearly twice the British rate [S10, pp. 194-195].

## East Indies Fleet, 1945

Operation Sunfish: Force 63 sailed at 09:00 on 8 April 1945 with Queen Elizabeth, Richelieu, the escort carriers Emperor and Khedive with Hellcats and Avengers, cruisers and destroyers; at about 02:00 on 11 April Queen Elizabeth, Richelieu, London and three destroyers detached, and at about 06:30 in position 6N 95-30E began bombarding Sabang with Hellcats of 808 Squadron spotting; ten Ki-43 fighters attacked the withdrawing ships at about 08:30 without result [S29]. Richelieu fired seven salvos and again silenced the coastal battery with her secondary guns [S10, p. 196; S16, p. 408]. Operation Bishop, 27 April to 8 May: Richelieu and Queen Elizabeth formed separate surface groups; Richelieu shelled the Car Nicobar airfields at 23,600 m with 80 main and 45 secondary rounds, then Port Blair from 17:30 to 18:07 on 29 April until her allotted main battery ammunition was spent, and returned on 2 May to fire 120 secondary rounds into the harbour [S10, pp. 198-199; S16, p. 412]. Queen Elizabeth's chronology records Force 63 dividing on the night of 10 to 11 April into Force 64 and Force 68 (Richelieu, Cumberland, Empress, Shah and destroyers) [S29]. Operation Dukedom (9 to 18 May): Richelieu and Cumberland were Group 3 of Force 61 with the destroyers Saumarez, Venus, Vigilant, Virago and Verulam, detached toward the Six Degree Channel; at 12:50 on 16 May Walker ordered Cumberland and Richelieu to support the destroyers, which sank Haguro before they arrived [S29; S10, p. 199; S16, p. 417].

## Casablanca, 8 to 10 November 1942: Jean Bart as a floating battery

The French had one light cruiser, nine destroyers, submarines, the El Hank battery and Jean Bart, moored at a quay with one working turret, against Massachusetts, Ranger, Suwannee and three heavy cruisers [S10, pp. 156-158; S16, pp. 209-210]. Jean Bart's fire control was the shore triangulation system of 1942 [S10, pp. 155-156]. Massachusetts came under fire from Jean Bart's 15-inch guns and replied at 07:40, the first US 16-inch shells fired against the European Axis; she silenced Jean Bart's main battery "within a few minutes" [S21]. NHHC's account: El Hank straddled Massachusetts first; Jean Bart's first salvo fell a couple of hundred yards from her; the "play ball" order came at 07:04; Massachusetts fired nine full nine-gun salvos, 81 rounds, and hit Jean Bart five times in 16 minutes, the first in an empty magazine and the last glancing off turret I's armour into the city unexploded, jamming the turret drive for eight hours [S22]. Jordan and Dumas: Jean Bart fired four two-gun salvos between 07:08 and 07:19, then smoke from the sortieing French ships blinded her; hits came at 07:25 through both armour decks into the empty 152 mm magazines, at 07:37 through the funnel to the armour deck edge, at 08:06 two shells, one on turret I pushing the glacis down and jamming it and one breaking up on the superfiring barbette, and at 08:10 on the quarterdeck through the sloped deck into a ballast compartment; two bombs at 07:18 had already damaged the port catapult and flooded the manual steering compartment [S10, pp. 156-158; S13, p. 81]. Workers cut away the jammed steel and the turret was working again at 17:24; only the magazine hit could have sunk her had the magazine been full [S10, pp. 158-159]. On 10 November, with the turret repaired but left looking damaged, she engaged Augusta from 11:41 to 11:51 with nine two-gun salvos, straddling three times and drenching Augusta's bridge with yellow dye [S10, pp. 158-159; S22]. At 15:00 nine Dauntlesses with 1,000-lb bombs, covered by eight Wildcats strafing the anti-aircraft guns, hit twice, wrecking the forecastle and the stern, starting fires put out by 20:00, and flooding until she settled by the stern with the turbo generator room under water [S10, pp. 159, 161; S22]. She fired 25 main battery shells in the action, lost 22 killed and 22 wounded, and her turret and 90 mm guns were still serviceable but without power [S10, pp. 159, 161; S17, pp. 158-166; S13, pp. 69-70]. NHHC's museum summary: the French "even repairing the guns of Jean Bart, eventually silenced by USS Ranger dive-bombers"; they surrendered on 11 November [S23]. The photographs 80-G-30616, 80-G-37325 and 80-G-31605 record the damage [S24; S25; S26].

## Conflicts between sources

- Richelieu's force at Cockpit: Force 69 [S29; S30; S10, p. 192] against TF 70 [S34; S40].
- Jean Bart hits on 8 November: five [S22] against five listed plus two shells on the quay [S10, pp. 156-158], and "five or seven" [S35].
- Time Massachusetts opened fire: 07:40 [S21] against the "play ball" order at 07:04 [S22] and Jean Bart firing from 07:08 [S10, pp. 156-158].
- 1943 refit dates: 30 January to August 1943 [S35] against 11 February to 14 October 1943 [S10, p. 150; S10, pp. 186, 188-189] and February to October [S38].
