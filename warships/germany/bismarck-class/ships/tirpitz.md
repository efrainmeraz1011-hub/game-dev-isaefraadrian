# Tirpitz

Status: partial (see gaps.md)

Second and last ship of the class, and by KBismarck.com's description the largest warship built in Germany [S19]. The facet files describe Bismarck unless they name Tirpitz; this note collects what a fetched source states about Tirpitz specifically.

The Bundesarchiv research guide lists her as launched 1939, commissioned 1941 and capsized in 1944 after a Royal Air Force attack, and holds the personnel files of Karl Topp and Hans Karl Gustav Meyer [S32]. Her dated sequence is in `08-service-history.md`.

## Was she the heavier ship

The sources fetched disagree, and none of them is a primary-tier source for the design figures.

| Source | Bismarck | Tirpitz | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| navypedia, standard displacement | 41,700 t | 42,900 t | 3 [S38] |
| navypedia, full displacement | 50,900 t | 52,600 t | 3 [S38] |
| KBismarck.com ship pages, standard | 43,978 metric tons [S1] | 43,900 metric tons [S19] | 2 |
| KBismarck.com ship pages, maximum | 53,486 metric tons [S1] | full load max. 53,500 metric tons [S19] | 2 |
| KBismarck.com comparison table, "Tirpitz (1944)" | standard 43,978 mt, full load 51,700 mt | standard 43,978 mt, full load 51,700 mt | 2 [S1] |

On navypedia's figures Tirpitz was about 1,200 t heavier standard and about 1,700 t heavier at full load [S38]. On the KBismarck.com ship pages she was 78 metric tons lighter standard and 14 metric tons heavier at maximum [S1; S19], and the comparison table on the same site repeats Bismarck's own figures for her [S1]. No tier 1 or tier 2 source fetched states the weight growth directly; see `gaps.md`.

Two dimensional differences are stated. Maximum draft: 10.61 m for Tirpitz [S19] against 10.55 m for Bismarck [S1]. Length: navypedia gives Tirpitz 241.7 m waterline and 253.6 m overall against Bismarck's 241.5 m and 250.0 m [S38], while KBismarck.com gives both ships 241.55 m and 250.5 m [S19; S1] and the US Naval Technical Mission measured her at 790 feet between perpendiculars and 821.5 feet (250.4 m) overall [S18, principal characteristics].

## Torpedo tubes

Bismarck carried no torpedo tubes and no mines [S15, §VII(1)]. Tirpitz received two quadruple 53.3 cm mounts, eight tubes in all, on the upper deck to each side of the ship amidships in section X, fitted at Gotenhafen in October 1941 along with an increase in the anti-aircraft battery [S19]. navypedia dates the same fit to September 1941 [S38, modernizations]. The tubes appear in her armament list as 8 x 53.3 cm in two quadruple mounts [S19]; their position in the section scheme is recorded in `05-compartments-and-deck-plans.md`.

## Other differences from Bismarck

- Machinery. Three Brown Boveri turbine sets against Bismarck's Blohm & Voss sets, and a cruising turbine running at 4,220 rpm to a shaft speed of 170 rpm that Bismarck did not have [S5, turbines and footnote 2; S38]. Maximum obtained 163,026 hp and 30.8 knots [S19], rounded to 163,024 hp and 30 to 31 knots in the 1944 comparison table [S1], against 150,170 hp and 30.12 knots in Bismarck [S1; S5]. navypedia adds that her real fuel rate exceeded the expected figure by 10% at full speed and 19% at cruising speed [S38, project history].
- Generators. Her diesel motor room No. 1 held Motorenwerke Mannheim RS 38 S serial 170094 driving Garbe-Lahmeyer alternator serial 376848, against serials 170093 and 377071 in Bismarck [S5].
- Fuel and endurance. 8,297 metric tons capacity [S19] against 7,780 metric tons in the comparison table [S1] and in navypedia [S38]. Endurance 9,280 nm at 16 knots, 8,870 nm at 19 knots, 6,693 nm at 24 knots and 4,728 nm at 28 knots [S19].
- Heavy anti-aircraft mounts. NavWeaps gives her an initial fit of six forward Dop. L. C/37 and two after Dop. L. C/31d, replaced in late 1941 by a uniform eight C/37 [S11, mount data]. navypedia states instead that she had all sixteen 10.5 cm guns in C/37 mounts from the start, with their higher aiming velocity, where Bismarck and every earlier German capital ship had C/31 [S38, project history].
- Light anti-aircraft. 20 x 2 cm in 1941 growing to 78 by 1944 [S19]. navypedia's steps: four single 2 cm C/38 added July 1941; those four removed and six quadruple mounts added September 1941; two more quadruples June 1942; two more March 1943; six singles removed and eight quadruples added June 1944 [S38, modernizations].
- Radar. Three FuMO 23 in 1941; three FuMO 23, one FuMO 27 and a FuMB 4 Samos in 1942; three FuMO 26, a FuMO Hohentwiel and a FuMO 213 "Würzburg-D" in 1944 [S19]. NavWeaps dates the foretop FuMO 27 tower with Timor and Sumatra antennas to about January 1942 and an enlarged 3 by 6 m mattress, probably for FuMO 26, to spring or summer 1944 [S14, Bismarck class]. navypedia lists FuMB 4, FuMB 7 and FuMB 6 electronic countermeasures suites added in September 1941 and June 1944 [S38, modernizations].
- Torpedo protection. The US Naval Technical Mission reported her system as designed to withstand about 660 pounds (300 kg) of German hexanite [S18, §3(b)], against the 250 kg of TNT KBismarck.com gives for Bismarck [S3].
- Complement. 2,608, of whom 108 officers and 2,500 men [S19; S38], against 2,065 as commissioned in Bismarck [S29].
- Ammunition. An anti-aircraft version of the 38 cm nose-fused shell with the Z.Z. S/60 nA time fuze and no windshield, about 1,740 lbs (789 kg), was supplied to her after 3 April 1944 and fired at the Lancasters of 12 November 1944 from 12,500 yards (11,200 m) [S9, ammunition note 4a].

## Condition at the end

On 12 November 1944 she lay at anchor in Tromsø fiord with about 3,000 tons of fuel and fresh water aboard, roughly 30 percent of a full load of about 10,000 tons, and with spaces in bow and stern still flooded from earlier attacks [S18, §2, §4]. Her displacement was calculated at about 42,000 tons, mean draft about 29.0 feet, GM about 12.0 feet, with a one degree list to port left uncorrected so as not to reduce reserve buoyancy [S18, §3(e), principal characteristics]. The 7,000 tons of liquid missing from tanks low in the ship had the effect of a removed low weight, and fuel tanks were not ballasted with seawater as a matter of custom [S18, §3(e), §4].

## Conflicts between sources

- Laid down: 2 November 1936 [S19] against 20 October 1936 [S38].
- Standard and full load displacement: see the table above [S1; S19; S38].
- Length overall: 250.5 m [S19] against 253.6 m [S38] against 821.5 feet, 250.4 m, as measured in 1944 [S18].
- Fuel capacity: 8,297 metric tons [S19] against 7,780 metric tons [S1; S38].
- Torpedo mounts fitted: October 1941 [S19] against September 1941 [S38].
- Heavy anti-aircraft fit: six C/37 forward and two C/31d aft until late 1941 [S11] against all sixteen guns in C/37 mounts [S38].
- Dead on 12 November 1944: 971 [S19] against about 1,200 [S18].
