# Bismarck class: sensors, fire control and communications

Status: partial (see gaps.md)

## Command posts and rangefinders

Main and secondary battery fire was directed from three command posts: the foretop, the forward post attached to the forward conning tower, and the after post; each carried a rotating dome with a Zeiss stereoscopic rangefinder and a FuMO 23 radar [S6, command posts]. Armoured communication shafts joined the posts to the computation rooms (Rechenstellen) under the armour deck forward in section XV and aft in section VII [S6]. The foretop post, station of the First Artillery Officer, stood about 31 m above sea level with a 10.5 m base rangefinder of up to 50x magnification and a 360 degree field; the forward post had a 7 m rangefinder with a field cut by the superstructure; the after post had a 10.5 m rangefinder [S6]. Each 38 cm turret had a 10.5 m rangefinder for local fire; the one in turret Anton was removed at Hamburg in the winter of 1940/41 because sea water fogged it at speed [S6, footnote 1]. The AVKS reported in May 1941 that the turret Anton rangefinder "still had not been done" and could be abandoned, and that the 10 m rangefinders in the lower turrets were of limited use because of their low eye level [S16, ¶105]. The central 15 cm turrets carried 6.5 m rangefinders [S6; S10, mount data note 2b]; they were not yet aboard during the AVKS tests [S16, ¶105].

The general data table lists 5 x 10.5 m, 1 x 7 m, 2 x 6.5 m, 4 x 4 m type SL-8 and 2 x 3 m rangefinders (source undated) [S1]. Prisoners told the Admiralty of 15 rangefinders, stereoscopic Zeiss instruments of 10 m base, 25 km effective range and 50x magnification in the foretop, forward and after positions and in each main turret; 3 m gyro-stabilised combined range-, direction- and height-finders of 15 km range and 25x in the four Flak posts; and 70 cm portable rangefinders for the light Flak; three rangetakers manned each 10 m and 3 m instrument and the ship carried 35 trained rangetakers under Kapitänleutnant Knappe (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(7)]. Ranges from the turret rangefinders were passed to the plotting rooms but ignored as unreliable [S15, §VII(6), (7)]. The AVKS gave the 10 m rangefinder a field of view of 4 degrees, narrowing to 2 degrees 11 minutes at 18x and 1 degree 12 minutes at 50x, and a full dome crew of four for the optical instrument and three for the radar [S16, ¶107, ¶108]. Two 3 m night rangefinders stood on either side of the Admiral's bridge [S6].

## Fire control computers and plotting rooms

Two plotting rooms served the 38 cm (S.A.) and 15 cm (M.A.) guns: the forward Artillerie-Rechenstelle in Section XV on the middle platform deck, and the after one in Section VII on the upper platform deck, each with adjoining change-over switch rooms (Artillerie-Hauptschaltstelle forward, Reserve-Schaltstelle aft) and valve amplifier rooms (Verstärkerräume) [S15, §VII(6)]. Each plotting room held two officers and 23 ratings split between S.A. and M.A.; the Siemens plotting instruments (Schusswertrechner), separate for S.A. and M.A., had a first portion worked by five men into which the values from the control positions were entered and a second portion worked by two men that refined the results ("R.V., H.V. and A.V." corrections) and passed them to the guns; each set was in charge of a Bootsmaat with earphones to the directors [S15, §VII(6)]. Ranges from the rangefinders were averaged by the "E-Messer-Mittler"; one rating worked the firing signal switches that sounded the turret klaxons and lit the signal lamps (white when loaded, red when fired) repeated in the plotting rooms; "Gegner-Geber" reported enemy details from aloft [S15, §VII(6)]. The after switch room was manned by seven gunnery artificers under Oberleutnant Hinz [S15, §VII(6)].

The AVKS found the computer stations roomy enough for a full crew and the switchboards clearly arranged; it asked that the order transmitters, opponent assigner and salvo pace clock be removed from the command posts, that the fire signal indicator's partial-salvo switching be changed from left barrels/right barrels to turrets A+B / C+D (and for the secondaries turret I / turrets II+III), and that the star shell guide unit (Lg-Leitgerät) be moved below decks to the computing station [S16, ¶50 to ¶54]. Target designation ran through Zielgeber C/38 target indicators in the foretop and forward station, aiming posts (Zielsäulen) and azimuth bearing instruments (Seitenpeilgeräte) [S16, ¶55]. Battery-less wall telephones were fitted as last-resort gunnery communication and the gunnery telephone switching followed the target-indicator axis rather than the fire-signal axis [S16, ¶60, ¶61]. A quadruple cathode-ray recorder for muzzle velocity measurement was installed but only the 15 cm barrels had been drilled for its probes [S16, ¶66 to ¶68].

Remote power control: RPC was fitted for elevation but not for training, and the elevation control was considered unsatisfactory in Bismarck [S9, mount data]. The AVKS measured an average elevation remote-control error of 7/16 degree with runs of 9.5/16 degree, found it sensitive to voltage fluctuations of a few volts, and reported that its calibration drifted repeatedly during the eight weeks aboard [S16, ¶10]. Manual elevation laying held to roll angles of about 3 to 4 degrees; at 6 degrees of roll re-laying was impossible [S16, ¶6].

## Anti-aircraft fire control

Four SL-8 (Stabilisierter Leitstand) AA directors each carried a 4 m rangefinder; two under spherical cupolas (Wackeltopf) stood either side of the foremast and two uncovered posts amidships aft [S6, footnotes 2 and 3]. Prisoners located them as Flak-Leitstand A (superstructure deck, starboard forward), B (superstructure deck, port), C (control tower on the Deckhaus deck abaft the mainmast, seeing above the after 10 m rangefinder) and D (after end of the Deckhaus deck, seeing below it), each with one officer (Flakleiter), a rangetaker and two ratings keeping the instrument on target; values went electrically to the Flak plotting rooms (forward in Section XV on the upper platform deck with the forward Flak switch room, aft in Section IX on the middle and lower platform decks) or directly to the guns [S15, §VII(6)(B)]. Kapitänleutnant Gellert commanded all AA from a gallery below the foretop director with Kapitänleutnant Troll as control officer for the heavy Flak; Oberleutnant Brandes controlled the light Flak from the foot of the mainmast and Oberleutnant Soltau the forward light Flak from the gallery below the forward director, where the two quadruple 2 cm mounts stood [S15, §VII(6)(B), (C)]. Barrage fire (Zonenfeuer) with pre-set fuse zones 1 and 2 was used against simultaneous attacks and dive bombers; controlled fire (Geleitschiessen) against single aircraft; with several aircraft the weapons were split into four 90 degree sectors [S15, §VII(6)(B)].

The AVKS battle roster for the main Flak battle station in the foretop gallery counted 23 to 25 men, including two Flak combat directors, eight order transmitters, four target-alignment crew and torpedo-danger observers, all without splinter protection [S16, ¶69]. Only two target designation devices (Zag) were fitted; four were requested [S16, ¶70]. The SL 8 directors suffered repeated failures during the tests, including a two-day shutdown of station B from a broken drive shaft, and the interaction of the lateral-lead and alignment remote controls pushed the target out of the rangefinder's 1.5 degree field at 40x [S16, ¶75, ¶79]. Flak computers: four Reg C/VI calculators were the final fit, two per computer station; the tests ran with two [S16, ¶82, ¶83].

## Gunnery method

KBismarck.com describes the opening method: three partial salvoes fired in quick succession at different ranges (for example Anton, then Bruno two seconds later, then Dora) so that all three were in the air together; corrections after the first group usually straddled with the second; then full or partial salvoes for effect [S6, shooting basis]. The AVKS described ranging with bracketing groups of fore and aft partial salvoes of four shots each (preferred) or turret salvoes of two shots, and fire for effect with fore/aft partial salvoes, full eight-gun salvoes being reserved for special cases because the shorter splashes hid the longer ones [S16, ¶3]. Bonomi notes that after the first salvo a ladder of 400 metre steps was fired (one short, one on, one over), then rapid fire on the straddling setting [S23, note 31]. The Denmark Strait sequence is in 07-operations-and-doctrine.md.

## Radar

Bismarck carried three FuMO 23 sets, one on each command post [S1; S6]. The rectangular "Matratze" antennas were about 2 m high by 4 m wide, working at 368 MHz (81.5 cm wavelength) with 9 kW output, and had an effective range of about 25,000 m; the display was an A-scope with no PPI, so the sets tracked one target at a time with poor bearing accuracy [S6, sensors]. The NavWeaps radar article says the added weight of a radar tower would have overloaded the rangefinder bearing motors, so both ships got enlarged towers for all three gunnery rangefinders housing FuMO 23 and the optics together, and that a fourth mattress on the front of Bismarck's conning tower, always covered by canvas in photographs, is disputed in the German literature [S14, Bismarck class]. Its table lists FuMO 21, FuMO 23 and FuMO 26 at 368 MHz [S14, table]. Prisoners called the equipment "D.T.", described mattress aerials 4 by 2.5 m (two on the foremast, one aft), said an officer and three specialists joined at Gotenhafen the day before sailing to work it, gave an effective range of 30 km, and said its ranges were not fed to the plotting rooms (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(14)]. The AVKS tested the "Em-II" against the cruiser Emden for range, accuracy and bearing, and rigged an experimental switch box with Siemens displays and a modified 800:50 hm gear ratio so radar range and bearing could be fed in parallel with the optical rangefinder to the gunnery computer; it recommended permanent integration and a hectometre scale on the cathode ray tube [S16, ¶114 to ¶116].

In service: the forward FuMO 23 was disabled by the blast of the forward turrets on 23 May 1941 [S20; S23, p. 1], after which Prinz Eugen took the lead to cover the forward sector with her FuMO 27 while Bismarck's aft set covered astern [S23, p. 1]; the war diary for 0228 on 24 May records Prinz Eugen taking over EM II measurement for the aft sector because Bismarck's instruments were unclear [S17, 24 May, 0228 entry]. Lütjens reported on 25 May that his own EM-2 instrument was prone to failures, especially during firing, and that the enemy's radar ranged at least 35,000 m [S17, 25 May, 0912 to 0948 entry]. Tovey judged from the destroyers' experience that Bismarck's R.D/F ranged accurately up to 8,000 yards at night [S24, ¶79]. Ensign Smith's Catalina crew concluded she had "some type of aircraft detector device" because she turned and opened fire before they broke cloud [S34, ¶10].

Tirpitz radar by date (source undated) [S19]: 1941 three FuMO 23; 1942 three FuMO 23, one FuMO 27 and a FuMB 4 Samos; 1944 three FuMO 26, a FuMO Hohentwiel and a FuMO 213 "Würzburg-D". The NavWeaps article dates the foretop FuMO 27 radar tower with Timor and Sumatra antennas to about January 1942, an enlarged 3 by 6 m mattress probably for FuMO 26 to spring or summer 1944, and the raising of the third AA director abaft the mainmast by 2 m for a Würzburg-C or D dish in the same period [S14, Bismarck class; S14, captions 5.4 to 5.7].

## Hydrophones and sonar

Bismarck carried two Gruppenhorchgerät (GHG) passive hydrophone arrays, one panel of 60 microphones on each side of the hull, with a maximum effective range of 26 to 40 km depending on water conductivity and background noise; Baltic tests were unsatisfactory, but in the North Atlantic bearings accurate to 1 degree at 30 knots from 30,000 m were expected [S6, sensors]. Prinz Eugen's GHG picked up propeller noises of two ships to port at about 0530 on 24 May 1941 before any radar echo [S21], and reported torpedo noises at 0603 that caused both German ships to turn away [S17, 24 May, 0603 to 0614 entry; S23, p. 9 and note 54]. No source fetched states whether an active sonar set was fitted in either ship; see gaps.md.

## Radio

Bismarck had three W/T offices: Funkraum A in Section XV on the starboard side of the middle platform deck, B in the same section on the port side of the upper platform deck, and C in Section IX on the battery deck, the last used in peacetime for broadcasting music and news and for non-secret signals [S15, §VII(15)]. The reconstructed war diary shows traffic through Group West's radio relay station "Anton" on short wave and the coastal short wave West, to which Bismarck switched on the night of 26 May when interference started on Anton [S17, 26 May, 2343 entry]. Short signals (Kurzsignale) used a code book whose limited vocabulary shaped messages [S17, 26 May, 2007 entry]; several messages of the morning of 24 May were never received at home because of propagation south and west of Greenland [S17, 24 May, 1200 entry]. Group West ordered direction beacons transmitted for U-boats on 852 m for 5 minutes at 0 and 30 minutes past each hour and for bombers on 443 kHz at 15 and 45 minutes [S17, 27 May, 0245 and 0459 entries]. The last message from Bismarck, at 0710 on 27 May, asked for a U-boat to collect the war diary; a survivor said the antennas had been shot away [S17, 27 May, 0729 entry]. Loudspeakers on the upper, battery and main decks passed orders and battle news [S15, §VII(4)]. VHF (U.K.) use was prohibited from 1400 on 24 May [S17, 24 May, 1400 entry].

Three meteorologists were embarked; the weather watch (Bordwetterwarte) was kept from the foretop and reported to Germany every three hours from 0300 German Summer Time [S15, §VII(16)]. A B-Dienst (radio intelligence) officer served aboard as Bordnachrichtenoffizier [S29]; B-Dienst reports on 24 May identified the approaching Hood from intercepted signals [S17, 24 May, 0537 and 0543 entries].

## Searchlights, mine defence and other sensors

KBismarck.com lists seven 1.5 m Siemens-Schuckert searchlights: one on the foremast platform, four beside the funnel and two behind the mainmast in section IX [S8]. Prisoners described the largest as 1 m, three on the foretop, one each side abaft the funnel, one each side abreast the mainmast and one further aft, with the searchlight centre and workshops in Section VII on the main deck (prisoner statements, 1941) [S15, §VII(18)]. The AVKS asked for power assistance on the splinter cowls of searchlights 2 and 3 [S16, ¶62]. A bow protection device (Bugschutzgerät), a tube about 2 ft in diameter and 13 to 14 ft long low in Section XXII, was used in mined areas; paravanes for bow and amidships had been exercised once in the Baltic [S15, §VII(17)]; six paravanes were carried on the upper deck [S8]. A smoke apparatus in a tower on the Deckhaus deck could not be used after the Hood action because the spanners had been mislaid; drum-shaped smoke floats of about 2 cwt were carried [S15, §VII(20)].

## Command spaces

The lower conning tower (Kommandozentrale) in Section XIV housed the Executive Officer and sat next to the main damage control centre on the upper platform deck [S15, §VII(9)]. The ship's command and Admiral's staff were on the bridge; the after control position doubled as a steering position [S15, §VII(19)]. No source fetched describes a combat information centre by that name; the plotting rooms and Kommandozentrale are the equivalents documented.

## Conflicts between sources

- AA director rangefinder base: 4 m [S1; S6] against 3 m [S15, §VII(7)].
- Turret Anton rangefinder: removed in winter 1940/41 [S6, footnote 1] against "still had not been done" and could be abandoned in May 1941 [S16, ¶105]. NavWeaps says all four turrets originally had rangefinders and Anton's was removed after water damage in winter 1940/41 [S9, mount data].
- FuMO 23 effective range: about 25,000 m [S6] against 30 km stated by prisoners [S15, §VII(14)].
- Searchlight size: 1.5 m diameter, seven lights [S8] against "largest 1 metre" and eight positions [S15, §VII(18)].
- Radar antenna size: about 2 by 4 m [S6] against 4 by 2.5 m [S15, §VII(14)].
- Officer in the after post: Fourth Artillery Officer (IV.A.O.) [S6; S29] against "3rd A.O." [S15, §VII(6)] for von Müllenheim-Rechberg.
