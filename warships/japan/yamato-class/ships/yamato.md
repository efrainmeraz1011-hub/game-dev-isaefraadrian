## Where Yamato differed from Musashi

- Main belt. Yamato's main side belt was 410 mm (16.1 inches); Musashi's was 400 mm (15.8 inches), which the Technical Mission called a negligible difference [S12, p. 17].
- Splash dye. Yamato was issued yellow dye for her Type 1 armour-piercing shells, Musashi green [S5].
- 1944 secondary rearmament. Both ships landed their two beam 15.5 cm triple turrets in 1944. Yamato received six additional twin 12.7 cm mounts in their place, giving twenty-four 12.7 cm guns; the mounts intended for Musashi were not available before she was lost [S7], [S1].
- Final light anti-aircraft outfit. Yamato ended with 152 25 mm guns in 50 triple and 2 single mounts after the refit begun 25 November 1944 [S1]. Musashi's last recorded outfit was 130 guns in 35 triple and 25 single mounts [S2].

## Torpedo damage, 25 December 1943

USS Skate hit Yamato on the starboard side near No. 3 turret at 10-05N, 150-32E. The hole ran about 15 feet down from the top of the blister and about 75 feet fore and aft between frames 151 and 173. The upper magazine of No. 3 turret flooded through a small hole punched in the longitudinal bulkhead by the caving in of the waterline armour, and about 3,000 tons of water came aboard, far more than the designers of the side protective system had anticipated [S1].

The Technical Mission worked the same hit from the commanding officer's despatch: a hole about 5 metres (16 feet) deep from the top of the bulge connection at the armour and 25 metres (82 feet) long between frames 151 and 173, with water reaching the upper magazine of No. 3 turret through a small hole in the longitudinal bulkhead. It estimated the charge at about 635 pounds of torpex and put the initial list at 2 to 3 degrees [S12, p. 9]. The mission concluded that the hit was about 4 feet below the surface in way of the main belt above the joint, that the joint would rupture, and that the first bulkhead inboard of the armoured bulkhead had ruptured [S12, p. 9].

Codebreakers decrypted the ship's own damage summary, which gave a hole from frame 163 to 170, 11 metres across above the bilge and 5.5 metres below it, penetrating the outer plates of the bilge [S1].

## Magazine bulkheads fitted after the Philippines

When Yamato was first commissioned there were no watertight fore-and-aft bulkheads between the magazines. They were fitted after the Battle of the Philippines for damage control [S11, p. 19].

## Loss, 7 April 1945

The Technical Mission interviewed survivors. By 6 April she was fuelled to 90 per cent of capacity and carried a full outfit of ammunition, including fuzed anti-aircraft projectiles for the 46 cm guns, at a mean draft of about 35 feet. The crew was veteran, about 2,400 officers and men, with reasonably high morale [S12, p. 11]. She sortied at 1500 on 6 April, set material readiness at 1800 with one third of the crew at battle stations and the remainder sleeping near their stations, and steamed at 20 knots [S12, p. 11].

On 7 April reveille and breakfast were complete by 0700. At about 1000 an uncertain radar contact brought the order "Prepare to go to General Quarters", and complete closure was set with all doors, hatches and ventilation closures shut, including the escape scuttles in the lower portion of the vertical watertight doors. Setting complete closure from the cruising condition took five to seven minutes. After 1200 speed went to 24 knots [S12, p. 11].

Hit sequence as the mission tabulated it [S12, pp. 12 to 15]:

| Attack | Time | Hits |
|---|---|---|
| First | about 1220 | Four bomb hits near No. 3 turret; two or three torpedo hits to port; list 5 to 6 degrees to port, corrected to 1 degree by counterflooding starboard voids |
| Second | about 1300 | Three certain torpedo hits to port at frames 143, 124 and 131, one to starboard near frame 124; No. 8 and No. 12 firerooms, the port outboard engine room and the port hydraulic machinery room flooded; list 15 to 16 degrees, speed not more than 18 knots; counterflooding brought the list to 5 degrees and used up all starboard voids |
| Third | about 1345 | Two torpedoes to port at frames 135 and 154, one to starboard at frame 150; the executive officer ordered starboard outboard firerooms Nos. 3 and 11 and the starboard hydraulic machinery room flooded, then the starboard outboard engine room; list 22 to 23 degrees |

Speed fell to not more than 10 knots with only the starboard inboard engine room left. All power was lost shortly after 1400, "Prepare to Abandon Ship" was passed, and the ship capsized at about 1420, exploding at about 120 degrees of roll [S12, p. 14]. Torpedoes were assessed at 600 pounds of torpex running at 18 to 22 feet [S12, p. 14].

The mission recorded a disagreement among survivors about the explosion. The assistant gunnery officer and the chief of staff attributed it to the fire aft reaching the after 15.5 cm magazines through the hoists. The executive officer argued that 46 cm high-capacity and incendiary projectiles racked vertically fell out nose first at 100 to 120 degrees of roll and detonated; all had fuzes installed, set on safe. A US Navy bomb disposal study of the fuze concluded that initiation that way was virtually impossible, and the mission judged the fire aft the more reasonable cause [S12, p. 15].

Casualty figures differ between sources; see the conflicts section of `08-service-history.md`.

## Wreck

On 1 August 1985 a Japanese team in the deep research submersible Pisces II located Yamato 1,410 feet deep in the East China Sea. The wreck is in two pieces: the forward section lies on its starboard side and the after section is bottom up. In the 1985 and 1999 surveys a bugle, eating utensils and other artefacts were salvaged, but no ship parts [S1].

The Yamato Museum in Kure displayed its own salvaged items recovered from Yamato in its 27th special exhibition, "Warships Sleeping on the Seabed: Yamato and Musashi", which opened on 24 April 2019. The museum director records, as an aside in his account of that exhibition, that a 25 mm twin machine gun was found during the Yamato seabed survey, which contradicted the plastic model kits then on sale [S36].
